# M10 part A: a dense table's frame work, a spike (2026-11-01)

Measured on Apple M1, macOS 26.6.2 arm64, machine `local`, Chromium headless=new, 1440×900 at DPR 2, cold, **3 runs each** (a spike, not the protocol's 10), reference and throttled (CPU 4×, Fast 4G), the wide shape only, the base first and the dense build right after in one session.

Raw: `2026-11-01-m10-a-spike-base.json` (the base, `just tall-table perf --shape wide --runs 3`), `2026-11-01-m10-a-spike-dense.json` (the dense build, the same command: its throttled scroll read in all 3 runs, its throttled fling timed out in each, so the file holds the failures), `2026-11-01-m10-a-spike-dense-throttled.json` (the dense build, throttled, one run, made to see the failure's message; none of its numbers are quoted here). The files' `label` fields say `spike-density-*`, their names at the run; renamed after. The perf script wrote `"build": "base"` in every file (it reads the features the host was built with, and the dense build had none); the two dense files' `build` field was edited by hand to `dense-spike` after the runs, the one edit to what the script wrote, so a reader of the field doesn't take the dense runs for the base.

## What changed

Nothing shipped. For the dense build, the table's constants were edited in place and put back after (`git checkout`): the row height 30 → 18 px (`ROW_SIZE`), the text 14 → 11 px (`TEXT_REMS` 0.875 → 0.6875), the cells' padding 8 → 6 px a side (`px_2` → 6 px, `CELL_PADDING` 16 → 12), and a float column 120 → 56 px (the wide shape's 500 columns are all floats). The header followed the row height, as it does today. The bytes aren't a reading: the edit isn't the feature.

## The spike's question

Whether frame work grows with the cells on screen in proportion, or something per frame (text shaping at a small size, the layout of ~4× the elements) makes a dense table cost more per cell than the base, which would need an optimization in `density`'s build. The research's "Frame-work risk" (root `docs/research/tall-table-features.md`, "`density`").

## Readings (reference, median of 3)

| Segment | Base: cells | Base: work p50 (recorder) | Base: presented / rAF | Dense: cells | Dense: work p50 (recorder) | Dense: presented / rAF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scroll | 308 | 6.89 ms | 124 / 145 | 1,128 | 21.14 ms | 65 / 149 |
| Fling | 308 | 5.13 ms | 183 / 202 | 1,128 | 14.62 ms | 123 / 204 |
| Sideways fling | 336 | 6.57 ms | 162 / 200 | 1,128 | 15.65 ms | 130 / 199 |

The recorder's p50 is `workRecorder`'s (each rAF that drew), the cross-check; the trace's own p50 for the dense scroll is 0.2 ms (below). Presented / rAF is the recorder's frames against the trace's rAF frames in the segment's window, each the median of the 3 runs. Not every rAF has something new to draw, so the base presents on ~85% of them while keeping up (0% late); presented frames alone don't say the table fell behind, only against the base's in the same segment.

Throttled (medians of 3, from `2026-11-01-m10-a-spike-base.json` and `-dense.json`): the base's scroll 28.81 ms (recorder 29.25), 65.52% late; the dense build's scroll 89.88 ms (recorder 88.73), presenting on 31 of 33 rAFs (each rAF waits on the last frame), and its fling timed out at the script's 30 s wait in every run.

## What it says

- **Work grows with the cells, about in a straight line.** A line through the two steady scrolls (308 cells at 6.89 ms, 1,128 at 21.14) is ~1.5 ms a frame and ~17.4 µs a cell; with two points it's a fit, not a finding, and the sideways fling (15.65 ms at the same 1,128 cells) sits off it. What it does say: nothing about 11 px text or 18 px rows made the work jump past the cells' count, so `density` needs no optimization of its own to be correct; its cost is the cells a host asks for.
- **At ~1,130 cells the table falls behind the display at the reference run,** in every segment: the steady scroll presents 65 frames in 2.47 s (~26 a second) against the base's 124 (~51), the fling 123 against 183, and the sideways fling 130 in 3.31 s (~39) against 162 (~49). The sideways fling fell behind with its work at 15.65 ms, under a 16.7 ms frame, so work per frame doesn't say where the table stops holding the rate; the frames presented against the base's do. Where between the base's ~310–340 cells and 1,128 that happens is part D's to find, at the protocol's 10 runs over a few dense settings.
- **The scripts' "late" frames missed it.** `perf.ts` counts late frames from rAF intervals, and Chromium kept firing rAF at 16.67 ms while GPUI drew on every other one: late 0% at 26 presented frames a second. The trace's frame work p50 (0.2 ms) is the half of the rAFs that drew nothing. A dense setting needs the presented frames counted against the base's in the same segment (the base presents on only ~85% of rAFs while keeping up, so a rate alone doesn't flag a slow table); part C adds it.
- **The throttled fling can't finish in the script's 30 s wait at this density.** Part C's waits follow the frame time, or the dense throttled segments are reported as not reached.

## Reproducing it

Edit the constants above in `src/state.rs` and `src/column.rs`, then:

```sh
just tall-table build
just tall-table perf --shape wide --runs 3 --label spike-density-dense
git checkout components/tall-table/src
```
