# Kirkwood

Kirkwood is a showcase: the asteroid belt as a scene to fly through, drawn from the asteroid table's own rows, with the table of those rows beside it, each moving the other. Named for Daniel Kirkwood, who found the gaps in the belt in a catalog of orbits, not through a telescope.

**The app draws the belt, plainly.** `crate/` and `web/` are the app: GPUI's first frame (the title, the time control, the table's handle), then the scene's module and the two files, each file written into the wasm's memory as it arrives; the scene draws every row with an ellipse on its own orbit from the elements where they lie, with the Sun, Jupiter on its orbit, and their names; the clock runs a year a second from 1976 to 2076; and the table is over every row in a panel behind the handle. Not built: the look, color by orbit size, the sky (every dot is the same pale dust), and anything that links a dot to its row. The throwaway spike (`spike/`: a Three.js scene under a transparent GPUI canvas with the tall table) decided the stack (below); it's never deployed, none of its code is carried forward, and it stays, as the viewer of the orbits' data (it draws from the real elements file, `data/elements.bin`), until the app's own scene draws the belt (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Planning").

## Kirkwood rules

These replace the measuring rules a showcase is exempt from (root `CLAUDE.md`, "Tiers": no budgets, no results notes, no measurement protocol) and add to the rest.

1. **Every dot is a row.** Nothing is drawn as an asteroid that isn't a row of the table, and a count on screen is counted from the rows. What the scene adds that isn't a row (the Sun, Jupiter, an orbit line, a marker) is plainly not a dot.
2. **Paint first, load the scene after.** Nobody watches a blank page while the data arrives (root rule 2).
3. **Say what's exaggerated or invented, on screen.** Dot sizes are exaggerated and the page says so; a marker asteroid is called a marker; a close-up body with no shape on file is labeled as illustration. No dense rock field: the belt is empty at true scale (the research, "What's honest to simulate").
4. **Color is data.** A dot's color is its orbit size; a dot without color (gray) isn't in the picked group; white is for what the interface adds (a drawn shape's outline, a marker and its path). Don't spend a hue on decoration (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Look").
5. **For a person to read.** Nothing on screen changes faster than someone can follow: a live cell refreshes at a rate chosen by looking at it, not as fast as the clock runs.
6. **No tour on load.** A visitor opts in through one-press actions, taken in any order or not at all.
7. **Handle the canvas tradeoffs in the open** (root rule 4), in `README.md`, once there's an app to have them.

## Stack

Decided by the spike (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Stack", which points to each part's own decision). Built so far: the rows, the clock, the interface, and the scene without its look; no sort flight, selection, or sky yet.

- **The scene:** Three.js on a canvas of its own, under GPUI's, loaded after GPUI's first frame. A deliberate exception to "logic in Rust, JS thin" (root `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Repo structure", the showcase tier). It draws only when the clock, the camera, the panel's width, or the window changed, at a resolution of its own, and every orbit is solved in its vertex shader, whose arithmetic the crate's tests run in 32-bit floats against its own 64-bit place for every row (`crate/src/shader.rs`: change the shader's `PLACE` and it together). The names beside the Sun and Jupiter are the page's text, between the two canvases, placed by the scene in the frame that draws them. The sky's stars are its own (JS): they aren't rows. Why: `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scene".
- **The engine:** Rust owns the orbital elements and their buffers (the scene's attributes are views over the columns), the clock and how far it goes, Jupiter's orbit (it isn't a row), the sort flight's progress, and the selection (a set of row indices, picked through the scene's camera). The scene asks Rust each frame (the clock, the panel's width, Jupiter's place); Rust calls the scene to hand it a wheel, and later to move its camera or hand it a group.
- **The interface:** GPUI through `shared/` (crate `seiza`), its canvas over the scene's and transparent wherever it drew nothing, with `components/tall-table/` in a panel on the right behind a handle. GPUI's hit test decides which canvas an input is for.
- **The rows:** held in Rust, as the bytes of the two files, each written into the wasm's memory as its response arrives and read where it lies (`crate/src/bytes.rs`: 32-bit words, so no `unsafe` and no copy); the table's delegate answers every page from them, and a filter to a group is the group's indices. No DuckDB, and no `engines/duckdb/`. Why: `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Data" and "Table".

just kirkwood see [--only paint,table,memory,belt,time,bodies,wheel] [--runs 5]   # the release build in headless Chromium: the first frame before the scene's module or either file is asked for; the table's rows, columns, first and last row; the wasm's memory against the files' bytes; the belt, its counts against the elements file's, from straight above on the epoch, and three rows alone against the crate's place; the time control; the Sun's and Jupiter's names; a wheel on each canvas; screenshots and seen.json in checks/out/ (about 3 minutes); run `build` first

The scene's knobs, on the page's query, for the checks and for looking (`web/src/scene.ts`): the camera, `?az`, `?elev`, `?roll` (degrees), `?dist` (au); `?scale` (the scene's pixels for each of the display's); `?only=<row>,<row>` (those rows alone, as discs `?size` CSS px across, with no Sun or Jupiter); `?marks` (lines from the Sun toward Jupiter, 60° either side of it, and opposite); `?band=<au>,<au>` (the orbit sizes that stay lit, `?bandGain` times as bright); `?alpha` (a dot's opacity). ## App gotchas

- **A file's address holds from `file_begin` to `file_end`, and a view over it doesn't:** the page makes a new `Uint8Array` over the wasm's memory for each piece of the response, since the memory growing detaches the last one.
- **The page's `fetch` for a file, and its import of the scene's module, start in `__kirkwood.firstFrame`,** which the crate calls after the first frame is presented. Never preload either or start one earlier: `just kirkwood see` fails on any request before the mark but the page's own.
- **What the scene is handed is a view over the wasm's memory, good until that memory next grows.** `setElements` asks the crate everything its frame needs first, then makes the views and draws (which uploads them) with no call into the wasm between; the scene never reads them again. Keep that order.
- **The scene never calls into GPUI from a call Rust made.** What it reads each frame are plain cells (`scene_years`, `scene_inset`, `jupiter_place`); `belt_drawn` and `scene_failed`, which update the host, are called from the page's own tasks.
- **The clock is moved by whoever reads it** (the scene every frame, the date four times a second), and the scene draws when the years it's given are new, so it draws nothing while time is paused, held, or stopped at its last day.
- **GPUI's canvas is transparent because of two things:** `web/src/alpha.ts` (the WebGPU context's alpha mode, set before the wasm starts) and the theme's `tokens.background` set transparent after `Theme::change` (`web.rs`). It leans on GPUI clearing to transparent and blending source-over, which it doesn't promise: after a gpui-kit bump, `just kirkwood see --only belt` and look at `checks/out/belt.jpg`.
- **The transparent `tokens.background` is read by more than the window's fill.** The tall table takes it as the text color over a cell's fill (`components/tall-table/src/paint.rs`, `text_over`), and gpui-kit components that paint with it (inputs, tooltips, outline buttons) would be see-through over the scene. Nothing here uses either yet: before the table gets a cell fill or a ramp, or such a component is added, give it a color of its own.
- **A name's size is measured when it's made and when a font arrives, never in a frame** (`scene.ts`, `measure`): a read of `offsetWidth` in the draw is a layout every frame.
- **An element over the scene must `occlude()`,** or a wheel on it also zooms the scene (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Input").
- **A color in the scene's own shaders is linear, and written through `#include <colorspace_fragment>`:** without it Three.js's `Color(0x…)` comes out darker and yellower than its hex.
- **A lost WebGL context isn't handled:** the scene's buffers would be uploaded again from views long detached (`PLAN.md`, open questions).
- **A published target is placed from its parent's corner** (`host.rs`'s `target`: `top_0`, `left_0`): an absolute child left at its static place starts inside the parent's padding, and its bounds come out shifted.
- **A script reads the table's layout once the panel has stopped sliding** (350 ms): the layout probe is the cells' place in the frame it was drawn.
- **The data's URL is under the app's base** (`import.meta.env.BASE_URL` in `main.ts`, the same base in `vite.config.ts`'s plugin): never write `/data/…` from the origin's root, which on the shared hostname is Tycho's.
- **The table has the keys from the moment its panel opens** (the host focuses it); a press elsewhere takes them away, as in any GPUI view.
- **Until the scene draws, what shows under the interface is the page's own background** (`index.html`), the color the scene clears to (`scene.ts`'s `PAGE`): keep the two the same.

## Spike gotchas

- **GPUI's canvas is transparent only because of two things on the host's side:** `spike/src/alpha.ts` (the WebGPU context's alpha mode, set before the wasm starts) and the theme's `tokens.background` set transparent after `Theme::change`. `?opaque` turns the first off; in a browser on WebGL2 (headless Firefox) it changes nothing, so `run.ts`'s control fails there. Why: `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Table".
- **An element over the scene must `occlude()`,** or a press on it also starts a shape on the surface under it (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Input").
- **The clock counts years from the elements' epoch** (2026-06-09), a year being one turn of a 1 au orbit (365.2569 days), so a row's mean anomaly needs no offset and its period is a^1.5; the date shown is the epoch plus those days.
- **The clock is moved by whoever reads it** (`advance_clock`: the scene each frame it draws, the date and the live column at their own rates), never by the scene's frames alone: landed in a against e the scene draws nothing, and time still runs (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scene").
- **The scene never calls into GPUI from a call Rust made.** Rust calls the scene inside a GPUI update (the pick's camera, the group's bytes, the outline, the fly-in), so what the scene reads back there are plain cells (`scene_years`, `scene_flight`, `scene_inset`); `fly_done`, which updates the host, is called only from the scene's own frame.
- **What Rust hands the scene is a view over the wasm's memory:** copied or uploaded before the call returns (`setPicked`, `setOutline`, `setElements`), since the memory growing detaches it.
- **H alone is the wrong brightness:** it's a body's brightness at 1 au, so the far, large bodies outshine the belt. The look divides by the squares of the distance from the Sun and from the eye (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Look").
- **The look's haze is taken before the bodies are drawn** (the Sun, Jupiter, the lines), so only rows and stars glow; keep that order.
- **A dot's size and opacity follow the camera's nearness** (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Look"); change one without the other and a flown-to group fades out or burns white.
