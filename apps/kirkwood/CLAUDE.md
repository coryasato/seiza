# Kirkwood

Kirkwood is a showcase: the asteroid belt as a scene to fly through, drawn from the asteroid table's own rows, with the table of those rows beside it, each moving the other. Named for Daniel Kirkwood, who found the gaps in the belt in a catalog of orbits, not through a telescope.

**The app draws the belt, with its look.** `crate/` and `web/` are the app: GPUI's first frame (the title, the time control, the action beside it, the table's handle), then the scene's module and the two files, each file written into the wasm's memory as it arrives; the elements come in blocks of rows, so the belt fills in as it arrives, the page counting the dots so far and time waiting for the last; the scene draws every row with an ellipse on its own orbit from the elements where they lie, its color its orbit size and its brightness from its H, through the look's passes, with the Sun, Jupiter on its orbit, and their names, and behind them the sky's stars, fetched last; the title's lines say what's counted, what's exaggerated, and whose the stars are, over the legend; the clock runs a year a second from 1976 to 2076; "Sort by orbit size" flies every dot from space into a against e, where the gaps open, and back, with what isn't a row leaving the picture on the way, the axes drawn under the dots as they land (a chip over each gap, the Hildas' and Trojans' names, and the count of rows in view); "Ride with Jupiter" flies the camera to straight above and turns the frame with Jupiter, which holds still to the Sun's right, with the Hildas and the Trojans lit, their names, and a dashed triangle through the three points in step with Jupiter; those two groups are moved for real, integrated under the Sun and Jupiter, so they keep their shapes across the clock's reach; a gap's chip on the landed layout is a button, and a press rides with Jupiter with a marker at that gap (white, in a ring, not a row), its path in the turning frame a closed white line, the belt dimmed under it, and a card under the title with the four gaps as chips, why the orbit empties, and three numbers from Jupiter's own orbit; the camera is a map's, a hand's to move in space (a drag pans along the ecliptic, a wheel zooms toward the pointer, a drag let go moving glides to rest, a drag with Shift held tilts and turns, and a double click zooms in; riding with Jupiter the hand has a camera of the ride's own, in the frame that turns), with a corner of its own, bottom right: a compass that shows its turn and tilt and flies it home when pressed, a hint of the controls that folds to a `?` button after the camera's first move, and behind the button the controls' list; the arrows pan, + and − zoom, H goes home, and `?` opens the list, while the table's panel is shut; and the table is over every row in a panel behind the handle. Not built: anything that links a dot to its row. A throwaway spike decided the stack (below); it's deleted, none of its code carried forward, and its screenshots are the record (`docs/spike/`; `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Planning").

## Kirkwood rules

These replace the measuring rules a showcase is exempt from (root `CLAUDE.md`, "Tiers": no budgets, no results notes, no measurement protocol) and add to the rest.

1. **Every dot is a row.** Nothing is drawn as an asteroid that isn't a row of the table, and a count on screen is counted from the rows. What the scene adds that isn't a row (the Sun, Jupiter, an orbit line, a marker) is plainly not a dot.
2. **Paint first, load the scene after.** Nobody watches a blank page while the data arrives (root rule 2).
3. **Say what's exaggerated or invented, on screen.** Dot sizes are exaggerated and the page says so; a marker asteroid is called a marker; a close-up body with no shape on file is labeled as illustration. No dense rock field: the belt is empty at true scale (the research, "What's honest to simulate").
4. **Color is data.** A dot's color is its orbit size; a dot without color (gray) isn't in the picked group; white is for what the interface adds (a drawn shape's outline, a marker and its path). Don't spend a hue on decoration (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Look").
5. **For a person to read.** Nothing on screen changes faster than someone can follow: a live cell refreshes at a rate chosen by looking at it, not as fast as the clock runs.
6. **No tour on load.** A visitor opts in through one-press actions, taken in any order or not at all.
7. **Handle the canvas tradeoffs in the open** (root rule 4), in `README.md`, once there's an app to have them.

## Stack

Decided by a spike, since deleted (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Stack", which points to each part's own decision). Built so far: the rows, the clock, the interface, the scene with its look, its color, and its sky, the sort flight, the frame that turns with Jupiter, the integration of the Hildas and the Trojans, and a gap picked; no selection yet.

- **The scene:** Three.js on a canvas of its own, under GPUI's, loaded after GPUI's first frame. A deliberate exception to "logic in Rust, JS thin" (root `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Repo structure", the showcase tier). Its camera is a map's (what it looks at on the ecliptic, its distance, its tilt, its bearing), moved by a hand through `web/src/camera.ts`: this canvas has no listeners, and the interface hands it each wheel, drag, and key (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Input"). It tells the interface its turn and tilt for the compass, from its own frames. It draws only when the clock, the sort flight, the camera, the panel's width, or the window changed, at a resolution of its own, and every orbit is solved in its vertex shader, as is each dot's flight to a against e, whose arithmetic the crate's tests run in 32-bit floats against its own 64-bit place for every row (`crate/src/shader.rs`: change the shader's `PLACE` or `FLIGHT` and it together). The names beside the Sun and Jupiter are the page's text, between the two canvases, placed by the scene in the frame that draws them, as are the ride's names and its dashed triangle, and a picked gap's marker, its name, and its path (an SVG line: 1.5 CSS px isn't WebGL's to draw). The ride with Jupiter is its camera's alone: one angle, Jupiter's longitude, and no buffer rewritten. It draws in linear light into a half-float buffer and reaches the canvas through the look's passes (`web/src/look.ts`: the haze, the bodies, the bloom, then one pass that quiets the picture under the page's text, tone maps, and adds the floor). The sky's stars are its own (JS, `web/src/sky.ts`, from `data/stars.bin`): they aren't rows. Why: `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scene" and "Look".
- **The engine:** Rust owns the orbital elements and their bytes (the scene's attributes are GL buffers of its own, filled from views over each block of rows as it arrives), the clock and how far it goes, Jupiter's orbit (it isn't a row), the color ramp (`crate/src/ramp.rs`: the shader's table and the legend's, one), the sort flight's progress and the axes it lands the dots on (`flight.rs`, `layout.rs`), how far the frame has turned to Jupiter's, the three points in step with it, and the two groups that hold there (`ride.rs`), those two groups' integration under the Sun and Jupiter (`integrate.rs`: the scene is handed their places when the clock moves), a picked gap's marker (`gap.rs`: its orbit, its place, its path in the turning frame, the card's numbers), and the selection (a set of row indices, picked through the scene's camera). The scene asks Rust each frame (the clock, the flight, the ride, a picked gap, the panel's width, where the page's text lies, Jupiter's place); Rust calls the scene to hand it a wheel, a press, a drag (its start, its moves, its end), a double click, an arrow or a zoom key, and the way home, each for the camera of a frame (the fixed one, or the ride's own), and later to fly its camera or hand it a group.
- **The interface:** GPUI through `shared/` (crate `seiza`), its canvas over the scene's and transparent wherever it drew nothing, with `components/tall-table/` in a panel on the right behind a handle. GPUI's hit test decides which canvas an input is for.
- **The rows:** held in Rust, as the bytes of the two files, each written into the wasm's memory as its response arrives and read where it lies (`crate/src/bytes.rs`: 32-bit words, so no `unsafe` and no copy); the table's delegate answers every page from them, and a filter to a group is the group's indices. No DuckDB, and no `engines/duckdb/`. Why: `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Data" and "Table".

## App gotchas

- **A file's address holds from `file_begin` to `file_end`, and a view over it doesn't:** the page makes a new `Uint8Array` over the wasm's memory for each piece of the response, since the memory growing detaches the last one.
- **The page's `fetch` for a file, and its import of the scene's module, start in `__kirkwood.firstFrame`,** which the crate calls after the first frame is presented. Never preload either or start one earlier: `just kirkwood see` fails on any request before the mark but the page's own.
- **What the scene is handed is views over the wasm's memory, good until that memory next grows.** `addRows` asks the crate everything its frame needs first, then makes the views (the blocks' addresses asked for before the first view) and writes them into its buffers with no call into the wasm between; the scene never reads them again. Keep that order.
- **The belt's buffers are the scene's own GL buffers, not Three.js's** (`GLBufferAttribute`, `scene.ts`'s `setBelt`): one a column, as long as the file's rows, written a block at a time. The belt is one draw, its range as far as the rows are in. A new per-row attribute is one more such buffer.
- **The scene is handed rows at most four times a second while the belt fills** (`main.ts`, `FILL_EVERY_MS`; the first block and the last at once). Hand over every block as it comes and a fast load's belt is whole 230 ms later (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scene").
- **A count on the page while the belt fills is of the rows the scene has drawn** (`belt_drawn(rows)`, which the page calls after each hand-over), never of the rows that have arrived. The clock is let go by the last hand-over alone, which waits for `file_end` to take the elements as one file.
- **The elements file's layout is in two places that must agree:** `crate/src/elements.rs` and `data/elements.ts`. A change to either is a new magic, a new manifest, and `just kirkwood test`.
- **The scene never calls into GPUI from a call Rust made.** What it reads each frame are plain cells (`scene_years`, `scene_flight`, `scene_space`, `scene_inset`, `scene_scrim_changes`, and when that has moved `scene_scrim`, `jupiter_place`, `scene_plot`); `belt_drawn`, `sky_drawn`, `scene_failed`, and `flight_hold`, which update the host, are called from the page's own tasks.
- **The clock is moved by whoever reads it** (the scene every frame, the date four times a second), and the scene draws when the years it's given are new, so it draws nothing while time is paused, held, or stopped at its last day.
- **The sort flight is moved the same way** (`flight.rs`: the scene every frame, the interface while the dots fly), and it is one number: the shader places each dot by it, so nothing is uploaded and no buffer rewritten. Landed, the scene draws nothing, whatever the clock does: time moves no dot on the axes.
- **The ride is the camera's and nothing else's** (`scene.ts`, `place`): the fixed frame's camera and the one straight above, turned to Jupiter's longitude, joined by `scene_ride`. Anything drawn through the scene's camera turns with the frame for free; anything placed in the window apart from it (GPUI's furniture) doesn't, and must ask the camera (`viewProjection`).
- **The ride moves the same way as the flight** (`ride.rs`, both over `tween.rs`): the scene every frame, the interface while the camera flies. A reading more than half a second after the last is a stall, and moves it a tenth of a second.
- **One action is pressed at a time** (`host.rs`, `toggle_sort`, `toggle_ride`): the press on one lets the other go in the same call, and both move at once. A third action joins the same rule. A picked gap isn't one: it's part of the ride (`pick_gap`), and "Ride with Jupiter" reads pressed with it.
- **A gap is picked only in the frame that turns** (`gap.rs`, `advance`): any reading with the ride at rest in the fixed frame lets it go, so the ride's press back and the sort's press need say nothing to it, and the marker leaves with the ride's own light (`gap::lit`). While riding, two presses let it go (`let_go`, 0.4 s): the card's picked chip (`pick_gap` with `in_card`; never the landed layout's chip, which is still under the pointer as it fades), and "Ride with Jupiter" pressed on again before the camera is back (`toggle_ride`).
- **The marker's orbit turns Jupiter's own mean motion times the ratio, never 1 / a^1.5** (`gap.rs`, `orbit_with`): a part in 2,000 off and its path never closes. The card's periods are from the same number; only the orbit's size is from Jupiter's a.
- **The marker's path is the crate's, in the frame that turns, asked for once a gap** (`gap_path`); the scene turns it by Jupiter's longitude and writes the SVG line only when the camera, the window, the panel, or the gap moved (`scene.ts`, `mark`, `pathFor`): riding, it holds still. On the camera's way there or back it isn't drawn (it would be written anew every frame): it waits for the ride to be at 1 and fades in on its own; the marker and its name don't wait, and `scene.marker()`'s path is empty until then. It closes after as many of Jupiter's orbits as the ratio counts (`gap::closes_in`), not one.
- **With a gap picked the ride's own additions leave** (the two groups' names, the triangle, their light: `scene.ts`'s `groups`, the shader's `uMarked`), and the title's sentence changes with them (`host.rs`, `GAP_LINE`): change one with the others. One gives its place to the other, never both at once: the ride's additions are gone by half of `gap::shown` (`groups_gone`), and the marker, its dim, and its card come in over the other half (`lit`), as the title's two sentences do (`host.rs`, `shows`).
- **The card starts under the title's lines, as the e axis does** (`host.rs`, `render_card`, `CARD_UNDER_TITLE`): text added to the title moves both. `see.ts` holds the card to 20 px under.
- **The sky leaves by a number of its own** (`scene_sky`: the flight's `space` times the ride's `sky`), and the title's sentence about the stars with it; riding, that sentence's place says what the turned frame brightens (`host.rs`, `RIDE_LINE`). Change the groups' light (`scene.ts`, `RIDE_LIGHT`) and it together.
- **The two groups are told apart in the shader by a and e** (`ride::bounds`, the shader's `uGroups`), in the file's floats; the crate's test reads the Trojans against every row's class. Never type a bound in `scene.ts`.
- **The Hildas and the Trojans are drawn apart, from places the crate integrates** (`integrate.rs`; `scene.ts`'s `integrate`, `uIntegrated`): the belt's shader turns their rows away by the same a and e the crate gathers them by (`ride::group`, `Elements::grouped`), so change the groups' bounds in `ride.rs` alone. Everything but a dot's place is the belt's shader and uniforms; a change to how a dot is drawn reaches both.
- **A row's integrated place is the same however the clock came to it** (steps on a grid from the epoch, only ever taken away from it: a clock that moves toward the epoch starts the rows over from there; between two steps, the two-body orbit from the last). Keep it so: nothing may step by a frame's own time, and no step is undone to save a few (a close pass of Jupiter doesn't undo to the same float). `place_of` integrates one row alone from the epoch, and `see.ts` holds the scene's rows to it; it answers by the row's group, whatever the scene is drawing (`ellipse_of` is where a row is drawn with `?integrate=0`, or while the rows catch up).
- **The places' address holds; a view over it doesn't.** `scene_integrated` is asked with the frame's other questions and returns an address; the view is made in `render`, at the upload, with no call into the wasm between.
- **After a jump of the clock the two groups are on their ellipses for some frames** (`web.rs`, `INTEGRATE_MS` of steps a frame; `stats.catchingUp`). A check on a date far from the epoch waits for it (`see.ts`, `withBelt`). Landed on the axes the rows are stepped and no place is worked out.
- **The two long studies in `integrate.rs` run only with `KIRKWOOD_STUDY` set** (a step's length weighed, which Hildas the resonance holds): `KIRKWOOD_STUDY=1 cargo test -p kirkwood --release <name> -- --include-ignored --nocapture`.
- **`just` hands a `;` in an argument to the shell:** `see`'s `--ways "a:;b:x=0"` is run as `node apps/kirkwood/checks/see.ts …` itself.
- **The ride's names and triangle are placed on the crate's three points** (`ride::points`), asked for when the years move while the frame is turned. A name's offset from its point is `scene.ts`'s (`RIDE_NAMES`), and `see.ts` holds a copy.
- **The camera's flight to the ride is between two ends that hold still** (`scene.ts`, `anchor`, `since`): the camera above is taken at Jupiter's longitude in the last frame drawn at rest, and Jupiter's turn since is summed a frame at a time and added on. Never fly toward the camera above as it is now: the shortest way to a moving end changes sides, and the camera jumps.
- **The groups' light and the page's sentence about it come in together,** over the second half of the camera's flight and only in space (`scene.ts`'s `uRide`, `host.rs`'s `shows`): change one with the other.
- **The camera is a hand's only in space, at rest in one of two frames,** and the interface decides which from the crate's own state, never the scene from its last frame (`host.rs`, `hands_frame`): the fixed frame's camera, or, once the frame turns with Jupiter, the ride's own (`scene::Frame`, handed over with every wheel, drag, and double click). On the way between the two, and with the dots out of space, an input moves nothing. The crate's state can be a frame ahead of the scene's picture (its own reading moves the ride on), so the scene takes an input only if the camera it last drew is at rest in that frame (`scene.ts`, `handOf`). Any button's release ends a drag.
- **The ride's camera is a map's numbers in the frame that turns** (`scene.ts`, `rideView`, `rideHand`; `stats.riding`): x along Jupiter's line from the Sun, its ground turned back by Jupiter's longitude as the camera was placed (`anchor`). It opens as board 2 has it and is forgotten once the camera is back in the fixed frame (`rideOpens`); the fixed frame's is the visitor's, as it was left (`stats.camera`; `stats.distance` is the camera's now, whichever it is). Its distance is kept as `rideZoom` times what the ride opens at, which follows the panel.
- **Riding, what holds still in the window is the Sun and the ecliptic's point under Jupiter on its line, not Jupiter:** its orbit isn't a circle (it goes in and out along the line) and is inclined 1.3° (tilted, it sits a few pixels off the plane). A check of the turning frame reads the point under it (`see.ts`, `seeTilt`).
- **The opening shot is a pose: rolled 34°, the belt from the bottom left to the top right, and a hand gives the camera no roll** (`scene.ts`, `OPENING`; `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Input"). A pan, a zoom, and the keys keep it; the first drag with Shift eases it out to a level horizon in a third of a second (`camera.ts`, `POSE`, `level`), since a tilt and a turn are a level camera's; the way home gives it back, and the compass's needle leans with it. An arrow key goes across the window, not along the rolled plane (`nudge`). The ride's camera has none. A check that wants a level camera says `?roll=0`. `stats.gliding` is true while the camera moves on its own (the glide, a zoom's way, the flight home, the roll's ease): a check waits on it.
- **A double click is two plain presses the surface itself took, in one frame, and its second starts no drag** (`host.rs`; `scene.rs`, `surface_press`): the click count alone also counts a first click on a chip that then leaves from under the pointer, or one made while the camera flew.
- **Shift is the key that tilts and turns; Ctrl is a pinch's, on a wheel alone** (`camera.ts`): a drag with Ctrl held pans.
- **The compass is a view of its own** (`host.rs`, `Compass`), as the date is: a turn redraws it alone, and the interface is drawn again only for its first showing and the hint's fold.
- **The compass is told, never asked:** the scene calls `camera_seen` at the end of a frame it drew, when the camera's turn or tilt moved (by a tenth of a degree while it moves on its own, exactly once it rests) or a hand first moved a camera (`scene.ts`, `tell`). Only from its own frames: a call the crate made (a wheel, a drag) marks the frame to draw and returns. Its needle is the camera's bearing less the one it opened with, so home is up; riding, the ride's own.
- **The corner is drawn only in space, once the scene is up** (`host.rs`, `corner_shown`): landed, its place is the count of rows in view's, and the keys do nothing. It fades with `flight::space`, takes no input from the sort's press on (`in_space`), and that press shuts an open list. Something new at the bottom right of the landed layout shares that place.
- **The hint folds for good on the camera's first move by a hand, or when the controls' list first opens** (`host.rs`, `fold_hint`; the page keeps it in `localStorage`, key `kirkwood:hint`). The flight home and a flight of the scene's own don't fold it. Where what the panel leaves is narrower than `HINT_ROOM` it is drawn as its button without being folded: a longer row of actions needs a wider `HINT_ROOM`.
- **The controls' list keeps clear of the table's handle** (`host.rs`, `handle_left`, measured when the handle is laid out): it is as tall as the handle is high up. A row added to it makes it taller; it must still fit under the title in a window 900 px high.
- **The keys are the interface's root's, by its focus handle** (`host.rs`, `key`): it takes the focus whenever nothing has it and the panel is shut, and hands it back when the panel shuts. With the panel open every key is left alone, so the table's reach the table. A key with Ctrl, Alt, or the platform's key held is the browser's.
- **An arrow key is a glide, a zoom key the double click's tween, and home a flight of the hand's own** (`camera.ts`, `nudge`, `zoomStep`, `home`): any press ends each, as it ends a glide, and `stats.gliding` is true through all three. A flight of the camera to a row or a group should be the hand's `home` with another place, so a hand takes it over the same way.
- **A picked gap's path breaks where it passes behind the eye** (`scene.ts`, `mark`): a place with no place on screen is a NaN in `pathDrawn`, and the line is drawn in runs. Anything else projected by hand through the camera's matrix needs the same test (`onScreen` has it).
- **A drag is the scene's from its press on the surface to its release, wherever that is** (`scene.rs`, `DRAG`; `host.rs`, `drag_listeners`: the window's own listeners, registered every frame). A new element over the scene needs nothing for it: occluding, it takes its own press, and a drag begun on the scene crosses it.
- **The camera's numbers are `camera.ts`'s `View`, and `scene.ts`'s `place` is the only reader that makes a camera of them.** A pan and a zoom lean on one fact, that the ray through a window position meets the ecliptic where `ground` says through the camera as placed now: change how the camera is placed (the view offset beside the panel, the roll) and both still hold, but move the camera anywhere else and they don't.
- **Near the horizon a pixel is tens of au:** a pointer holds a point of the ecliptic only within `REACH` camera distances along the plane. Past it a drag holds the point at the reach, and a wheel zooms toward what the camera looks at, as over the sky. What the camera looks at stays within `AT_MOST` of the Sun.
- **A glide's speed is read from the page clock when the crate hands each move over,** not from the events' own times, which GPUI doesn't pass on: moves that arrive bunched after a long frame read as faster than the hand was.
- **With time running, a published target beside the date can be a few pixels stale:** the date is a view of its own, and when it alone redraws at a new width the row of actions moves without the interface publishing their bounds again. `see.ts` reads an action's fill from a strip across its middle, never beside an edge (`actionFill`, `actionFills`).
- **The press pauses time, and the press back gives it back** only if the time control wasn't pressed between (`clock.rs`, `pause_for_sort`, `resume_after_sort`; `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scene").
- **The axes are the crate's** (`layout.rs`): the shader is handed their ends and where they are in the window, already in clip space (`Plot::clip`, which the crate's test of the flight takes too), and anything drawn on them (GPUI's furniture) asks the same `Plot`. Never type an axis's end or a gap's place in `scene.ts` or `host.rs`. A row is in view by a comparison in 32-bit floats, the shader's own, so the rows counted are the rows landed.
- **The axes' furniture is GPUI's, on the crate's plot** (`host.rs`, `render_furniture`; `layout.rs`'s ticks, chips, and groups): every place asks the `Plot` the shader lands the dots on, with the panel's width in it. It takes no input and doesn't occlude, so a press or a wheel on it reaches the surface under it; but the chips, which are buttons and occlude. `Plot::chips` keeps them apart and inside the plot, and gives none in a plot too narrow for four in a row: the line over them and their drops go with them. Its opacity is the flight's (`flight::axes`, `flight::landed`): `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Interface".

- **A check reads a moving thing's pixels on a held clock:** with time running the marker has moved on by the screenshot (`see.ts`, `marked`). And `ride`'s pressed sequence uses most of the clock's 50 s of reach from the epoch: on a busy machine the clock ends first, and its last step fails; run it again alone.
