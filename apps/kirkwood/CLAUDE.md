# Kirkwood

Kirkwood is a showcase: the asteroid belt as a scene to fly through, drawn from the asteroid table's own rows, with the table of those rows beside it, each moving the other. Named for Daniel Kirkwood, who found the gaps in the belt in a catalog of orbits, not through a telescope.

**The app draws the belt, with its look.** `crate/` and `web/` are the app: GPUI's first frame (the title, the time control, the action beside it, the table's handle), then the scene's module and the two files, each file written into the wasm's memory as it arrives; the elements come in blocks of rows, so the belt fills in as it arrives, the page counting the dots so far and time waiting for the last; the scene draws every row with an ellipse on its own orbit from the elements where they lie, its color its orbit size and its brightness from its H, through the look's passes, with the Sun, Jupiter on its orbit, and their names, and behind them the sky's stars, fetched last; the title's lines say what's counted, what's exaggerated, and whose the stars are, over the legend; the clock runs a year a second from 1976 to 2076; "Sort by orbit size" flies every dot from space into a against e, where the gaps open, and back, with what isn't a row leaving the picture on the way, the axes drawn under the dots as they land (a chip over each gap, the Hildas' and Trojans' names, and the count of rows in view); and the table is over every row in a panel behind the handle. Not built: anything that links a dot to its row. A throwaway spike decided the stack (below); it's deleted, none of its code carried forward, and its screenshots are the record (`docs/spike/`; `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Planning").

## Kirkwood rules

These replace the measuring rules a showcase is exempt from (root `CLAUDE.md`, "Tiers": no budgets, no results notes, no measurement protocol) and add to the rest.

1. **Every dot is a row.** Nothing is drawn as an asteroid that isn't a row of the table, and a count on screen is counted from the rows. What the scene adds that isn't a row (the Sun, Jupiter, an orbit line, a marker) is plainly not a dot.
2. **Paint first, load the scene after.** Nobody watches a blank page while the data arrives (root rule 2).
3. **Say what's exaggerated or invented, on screen.** Dot sizes are exaggerated and the page says so; a marker asteroid is called a marker; a close-up body with no shape on file is labeled as illustration. No dense rock field: the belt is empty at true scale (the research, "What's honest to simulate").
4. **Color is data.** A dot's color is its orbit size; a dot without color (gray) isn't in the picked group; white is for what the interface adds (a drawn shape's outline, a marker and its path). Don't spend a hue on decoration (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Look").
5. **For a person to read.** Nothing on screen changes faster than someone can follow: a live cell refreshes at a rate chosen by looking at it, not as fast as the clock runs.
6. **No tour on load.** A visitor opts in through one-press actions, taken in any order or not at all.
7. **Handle the canvas tradeoffs in the open** (root rule 4), in `README.md`, once there's an app to have them.

## Stack

Decided by a spike, since deleted (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Stack", which points to each part's own decision). Built so far: the rows, the clock, the interface, the scene with its look, its color, and its sky, and the sort flight; no selection yet.

- **The scene:** Three.js on a canvas of its own, under GPUI's, loaded after GPUI's first frame. A deliberate exception to "logic in Rust, JS thin" (root `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Repo structure", the showcase tier). It draws only when the clock, the sort flight, the camera, the panel's width, or the window changed, at a resolution of its own, and every orbit is solved in its vertex shader, as is each dot's flight to a against e, whose arithmetic the crate's tests run in 32-bit floats against its own 64-bit place for every row (`crate/src/shader.rs`: change the shader's `PLACE` or `FLIGHT` and it together). The names beside the Sun and Jupiter are the page's text, between the two canvases, placed by the scene in the frame that draws them. It draws in linear light into a half-float buffer and reaches the canvas through the look's passes (`web/src/look.ts`: the haze, the bodies, the bloom, then one pass that quiets the picture under the page's text, tone maps, and adds the floor). The sky's stars are its own (JS, `web/src/sky.ts`, from `data/stars.bin`): they aren't rows. Why: `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scene" and "Look".
- **The engine:** Rust owns the orbital elements and their bytes (the scene's attributes are GL buffers of its own, filled from views over each block of rows as it arrives), the clock and how far it goes, Jupiter's orbit (it isn't a row), the color ramp (`crate/src/ramp.rs`: the shader's table and the legend's, one), the sort flight's progress and the axes it lands the dots on (`flight.rs`, `layout.rs`), and the selection (a set of row indices, picked through the scene's camera). The scene asks Rust each frame (the clock, the flight, the panel's width, where the page's text lies, Jupiter's place); Rust calls the scene to hand it a wheel, and later to move its camera or hand it a group.
- **The interface:** GPUI through `shared/` (crate `seiza`), its canvas over the scene's and transparent wherever it drew nothing, with `components/tall-table/` in a panel on the right behind a handle. GPUI's hit test decides which canvas an input is for.
- **The rows:** held in Rust, as the bytes of the two files, each written into the wasm's memory as its response arrives and read where it lies (`crate/src/bytes.rs`: 32-bit words, so no `unsafe` and no copy); the table's delegate answers every page from them, and a filter to a group is the group's indices. No DuckDB, and no `engines/duckdb/`. Why: `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Data" and "Table".

## App gotchas

- **A file's address holds from `file_begin` to `file_end`, and a view over it doesn't:** the page makes a new `Uint8Array` over the wasm's memory for each piece of the response, since the memory growing detaches the last one.
- **The page's `fetch` for a file, and its import of the scene's module, start in `__kirkwood.firstFrame`,** which the crate calls after the first frame is presented. Never preload either or start one earlier: `just kirkwood see` fails on any request before the mark but the page's own.
- **What the scene is handed is views over the wasm's memory, good until that memory next grows.** `addRows` asks the crate everything its frame needs first, then makes the views (the blocks' addresses asked for before the first view) and writes them into its buffers with no call into the wasm between; the scene never reads them again. Keep that order.
- **The belt's buffers are the scene's own GL buffers, not Three.js's** (`GLBufferAttribute`, `scene.ts`'s `setBelt`): one a column, as long as the file's rows, written a block at a time. The belt is one draw, its range as far as the rows are in. A new per-row attribute is one more such buffer.
- **The scene is handed rows at most four times a second while the belt fills** (`main.ts`, `FILL_EVERY_MS`; the first block and the last at once). Hand over every block as it comes and a fast load's belt is whole 230 ms later (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scene").
- **A count on the page while the belt fills is of the rows the scene has drawn** (`belt_drawn(rows)`, which the page calls after each hand-over), never of the rows that have arrived. The clock is let go by the last hand-over alone, which waits for `file_end` to take the elements as one file.
- **The elements file's layout is in two places that must agree:** `crate/src/elements.rs` and `data/elements.ts`. A change to either is a new magic, a new manifest, and `just kirkwood test`.
- **The scene never calls into GPUI from a call Rust made.** What it reads each frame are plain cells (`scene_years`, `scene_flight`, `scene_space`, `scene_inset`, `scene_scrim_changes`, and when that has moved `scene_scrim`, `jupiter_place`, `scene_plot`); `belt_drawn`, `sky_drawn`, `scene_failed`, and `flight_hold`, which update the host, are called from the page's own tasks.
- **The clock is moved by whoever reads it** (the scene every frame, the date four times a second), and the scene draws when the years it's given are new, so it draws nothing while time is paused, held, or stopped at its last day.
- **The sort flight is moved the same way** (`flight.rs`: the scene every frame, the interface while the dots fly), and it is one number: the shader places each dot by it, so nothing is uploaded and no buffer rewritten. Landed, the scene draws nothing, whatever the clock does: time moves no dot on the axes.
- **The press pauses time, and the press back gives it back** only if the time control wasn't pressed between (`clock.rs`, `pause_for_sort`, `resume_after_sort`; `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scene").
- **The axes are the crate's** (`layout.rs`): the shader is handed their ends and where they are in the window, already in clip space (`Plot::clip`, which the crate's test of the flight takes too), and anything drawn on them (GPUI's furniture) asks the same `Plot`. Never type an axis's end or a gap's place in `scene.ts` or `host.rs`. A row is in view by a comparison in 32-bit floats, the shader's own, so the rows counted are the rows landed.
- **The axes' furniture is GPUI's, on the crate's plot** (`host.rs`, `render_furniture`; `layout.rs`'s ticks, chips, and groups): every place asks the `Plot` the shader lands the dots on, with the panel's width in it. It takes no input and doesn't occlude, so a press or a wheel on it reaches the surface under it; a chip that becomes a button must occlude. Its opacity is the flight's (`flight::axes`, `flight::landed`): `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Interface".
- **The e axis starts under the title's lines** where they end below the plot's top (`host.rs` reads the bounds the title measured for the scene's quiet), and the title asks for one more frame when those bounds move while the axes show. Text added to the title moves the axis's start; `see.ts` holds it to 8 px under the title.
- **What's said of the landed dots needs a belt:** the chips, the names, the line over the chips, and the count are drawn only once the scene has drawn rows; the axes alone stand without (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Interface").
- **A published target is never withdrawn:** its last bounds stay in `__seizaTargets` after its element stops being drawn, so a check of what's drawn reads a fresh page (`PLAN.md`, open questions).
- **A published target goes beside a bordered box, not in it:** an absolute child fills the box inside its border, a pixel short all round (`host.rs`, a gap's chip).
- **What isn't a row leaves the picture by one number** (`flight::space`, the scene's `leave`): the sky, the Sun, Jupiter, its orbit, the names, the haze, and the page's line about the stars. Anything else the scene adds to space joins `leave`, or it stays over the axes.
- **A wheel is the scene's only in space:** the interface hands none over while the dots are headed for the axes, on them, or on their way back (`host.rs`), and the way back from a sort is never turned down, whatever failed since the press.
- **The haze is off on the axes** (`look.ts`, `setHaze`): it lights the gaps (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Look").
- **The Sun, Jupiter, and its orbit's line are drawn in that order by `renderOrder`:** all three are transparent, which Three.js sorts far to near, so without it the line crosses Jupiter's disc on the far half of its orbit.
- **A fading run of text is a highlight's `fade_out`,** not a clear color: a highlight's color is blended over the text's own (`host.rs`, the line about the stars).
- **GPUI's canvas is transparent because of two things:** `web/src/alpha.ts` (the WebGPU context's alpha mode, set before the wasm starts) and the theme's `tokens.background` set transparent after `Theme::change` (`web.rs`). It leans on GPUI clearing to transparent and blending source-over, which it doesn't promise: after a gpui-kit bump, `just kirkwood see --only belt` and look at `checks/out/belt.jpg`.
- **The transparent `tokens.background` is read by more than the window's fill.** The tall table takes it as the text color over a cell's fill (`components/tall-table/src/paint.rs`, `text_over`), and gpui-kit components that paint with it (inputs, tooltips, outline buttons) would be see-through over the scene. Nothing here uses either yet: before the table gets a cell fill or a ramp, or such a component is added, give it a color of its own.
- **A name's size is measured when it's made and when a font arrives, never in a frame** (`scene.ts`, `measure`): a read of `offsetWidth` in the draw is a layout every frame.
- **An element over the scene must `occlude()`,** or a wheel on it also zooms the scene (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Input").
- **Every shader of the scene writes linear light, into the look's buffer; only the look's last pass writes the display's values** (its tone map, then `#include <colorspace_fragment>`). A shader that draws to the canvas itself would need both; none does.
- **The look's haze is taken before the bodies are drawn** (the Sun, Jupiter, the lines), so only rows and stars glow; keep that order (`look.ts`).
- **A dot's size and its opacity both follow the camera's nearness** (`scene.ts`, `render`); change one without the other and a near view fades out or burns white.
- **H alone is the wrong brightness, and a row with none is NaN:** the shader divides a row's flux by the squares of its distance from the Sun and from the eye, and finds a NaN by the float's bits, never by a comparison (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Look").
- **The Sun's glow is a square that faces the camera, not a point:** a point is dropped whole when its center leaves the window, and is only as big as the GPU allows (`scene.ts`, `SUN_VERTEX`). Anything else drawn wide goes the same way.
- **A GPU that can't draw into a half-float buffer gets 8 bits and no bloom** (`look.ts`): every dot is still drawn, nothing is brighter than white. Not seen on a real device; no check covers it.
- **The ramp is the crate's** (`ramp.rs`): the shader's stops and the legend's slices come from the one table. Never type a tint in `scene.ts`.
- **Rows drawn alone (`?only`) skip the haze, the bloom, the sky, and the bodies,** so a disc found in a screenshot is the disc: `see.ts`'s place check leans on it.
- **The scene quiets its picture where the title's lines lie** (`host.rs` measures them, `scene_scrim`). Text added over the scene elsewhere has no quiet under it: give it a fill, or widen what's measured.
- **The sky's file is the page's, not the crate's:** fetched whole after the rows file and the belt, never waited on, and a failure is the console's alone. The page's line about the stars shows only once they're drawn (`sky_drawn`).
- **A lost WebGL context isn't handled:** the belt's buffers are gone with it, and nothing fills them again (`PLAN.md`, open questions).
- **A published target is placed from its parent's corner** (`host.rs`'s `target`: `top_0`, `left_0`): an absolute child left at its static place starts inside the parent's padding, and its bounds come out shifted.
- **A script reads the table's layout once the panel has stopped sliding** (350 ms): the layout probe is the cells' place in the frame it was drawn.
- **The data's URL is under the app's base** (`import.meta.env.BASE_URL` in `main.ts`, the same base in `vite.config.ts`'s plugin): never write `/data/…` from the origin's root, which on the shared hostname is Tycho's.
- **The table has the keys from the moment its panel opens** (the host focuses it); a press elsewhere takes them away, as in any GPUI view.
- **Until the scene draws, what shows under the interface is the page's own background** (`index.html`), the color the look's floor tints the scene with (`scene.ts`'s `PAGE`): keep the two the same.
