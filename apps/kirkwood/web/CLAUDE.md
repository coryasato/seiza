# The host and the scene (`web/`)

The Vite host on `shared-web/` and the scene it loads after the first frame: Three.js, its camera, its look, and its sky. Claude Code loads this file when a file under `web/` is read. Kirkwood's rules and the map of its folders are in `apps/kirkwood/CLAUDE.md`, the repo's in the root `CLAUDE.md`; both apply here. Paths are relative to `apps/kirkwood/`.

## Architecture

- **The scene:** Three.js on a canvas of its own, under GPUI's, loaded after GPUI's first frame. A deliberate exception to "logic in Rust, JS thin" (root `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Repo structure", the showcase tier). Its camera is a map's (what it looks at on the ecliptic, its distance, its tilt, its bearing), moved by a hand through `web/src/camera.ts`: this canvas has no listeners, and the interface hands it each wheel, drag, and key (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Input"). It tells the interface its turn and tilt for the compass, from its own frames. It draws only when the clock, the sort flight, the camera, the panel's width, or the window changed, at a resolution of its own, and every orbit is solved in its vertex shader, as is each dot's flight to a against e, whose arithmetic the crate's tests run in 32-bit floats against its own 64-bit place for every row (`crate/src/shader.rs`: change the shader's `PLACE` or `FLIGHT` and it together). The names beside the Sun and Jupiter are the page's text, between the two canvases, placed by the scene in the frame that draws them, as are the ride's names and its dashed triangle, and a picked gap's marker, its name, and its path (an SVG line: 1.5 CSS px isn't WebGL's to draw). The ride with Jupiter is its camera's alone: one angle, Jupiter's longitude, and no buffer rewritten. It draws in linear light into a half-float buffer and reaches the canvas through the look's passes (`web/src/look.ts`: the haze, the bodies, the bloom, then one pass that quiets the picture under the page's text, tone maps, and adds the floor). The sky's stars are its own (JS, `web/src/sky.ts`, from `data/stars.bin`): they aren't rows. Why: `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scene" and "Look".

## The scene's knobs

The scene's knobs, on the page's query, for the checks and for looking (`web/src/knobs.ts`; `scene.ts` and `look.ts` say what each does): the camera, `?az`, `?elev`, `?roll` (degrees), `?dist` (au), `?at=<x>,<y>` (what it looks at, on the ecliptic, au); `?scale` (the scene's pixels for each of the display's); `?only=<row>,<row>` (those rows alone, as plain discs `?size` CSS px across, with no Sun, Jupiter, sky, haze, or bloom; `?hard=0` draws them as the belt's dots are); `?marks` (lines from the Sun toward Jupiter, 60° either side of it, and opposite); `?band=<au>,<au>` (the orbit sizes that stay lit, `?bandGain` times as bright); `?alpha` (a dot's opacity); `?integrate=0` (the Hildas and the Trojans left on their 2026 ellipses, for the checks to set the integration against); and a part of the look off, `?sky=0`, `?haze=0`, `?bloom=0`, `?scrim=0`, with `?bloomScale` (the bloom's resolution, of the buffer's). For a screenshot: `?gap=<0 to 3>` (a gap held picked, with `?ride=1`). Chosen, and left for looking: `?markerE` (the marker's eccentricity, 0.25) and `?markerDim` (the opacity a picked gap leaves the belt, of what the ride leaves it, 0.6). Chosen, and left for looking: `?glide` (the glide's time constant, ms; 220). Chosen from screenshots, and left for looking: `?gray` (what a drawn group leaves of a gray dot's opacity; 0.5). For the checks: `?fly=0` (a group picked leaves the camera and the panel as they are). To be looked at, not yet chosen by eye: `?found` (how far from a found row the camera's flight ends, au; 2). To be looked at, not yet chosen: `?rideSky=<0 to 1>` (how much of the sky the frame that turns with Jupiter keeps, turning with it; none as it ships, and the page's sentence about the stars doesn't follow the knob). Chosen by the owner's eye, and left for looking: `?glass=<0 to 1>` (what's left of the table's panel's fill: 0.2 as it ships, the panel glass; at 1 it is opaque and nothing is blurred) and `?glassBlur` (the blur behind it, CSS px; 8) (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Table"). A knob that isn't a number is the page's error.

## Gotchas

- **A file's address holds from `file_begin` to `file_end`, and a view over it doesn't:** the page makes a new `Uint8Array` over the wasm's memory for each piece of the response, since the memory growing detaches the last one.
- **The page's `fetch` for a file, and its import of the scene's module, start in `__kirkwood.firstFrame`,** which the crate calls after the first frame is presented. Never preload either or start one earlier: `just kirkwood see` fails on any request before the mark but the page's own.
- **What the scene is handed is views over the wasm's memory, good until that memory next grows.** `addRows` asks the crate everything its frame needs first, then makes the views (the blocks' addresses asked for before the first view) and writes them into its buffers with no call into the wasm between; the scene never reads them again. Keep that order.
- **The belt's buffers are the scene's own GL buffers, not Three.js's** (`GLBufferAttribute`, `scene.ts`'s `setBelt`): one a column, as long as the file's rows, written a block at a time. The belt is one draw, its range as far as the rows are in. A new per-row attribute is one more such buffer.
- **The scene is handed rows at most four times a second while the belt fills** (`main.ts`, `FILL_EVERY_MS`; the first block and the last at once). Hand over every block as it comes and a fast load's belt is whole 230 ms later (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scene").
- **A count on the page while the belt fills is of the rows the scene has drawn** (`belt_drawn(rows)`, which the page calls after each hand-over), never of the rows that have arrived. The clock is let go by the last hand-over alone, which waits for `file_end` to take the elements as one file.
- **The scene never calls into GPUI from a call Rust made.** What it reads each frame are plain cells (`scene_years`, `scene_flight`, `scene_space`, `scene_inset`, `scene_scrim_changes`, and when that has moved `scene_scrim`, `jupiter_place`, `scene_plot`); `belt_drawn`, `sky_drawn`, `scene_failed`, and `flight_hold`, which update the host, are called from the page's own tasks.
- **The ride is the camera's and nothing else's** (`scene.ts`, `place`): the fixed frame's camera and the one straight above, turned to Jupiter's longitude, joined by `scene_ride`. Anything drawn through the scene's camera turns with the frame for free; anything placed in the window apart from it (GPUI's furniture) doesn't, and must ask the camera (`viewProjection`).
- **The marker's path is the crate's, in the frame that turns, asked for once a gap** (`gap_path`); the scene turns it by Jupiter's longitude and writes the SVG line only when the camera, the window, the panel, or the gap moved (`scene.ts`, `mark`, `pathFor`): riding, it holds still. On the camera's way there or back it isn't drawn (it would be written anew every frame): it waits for the ride to be at 1 and fades in on its own; the marker and its name don't wait, and `scene.marker()`'s path is empty until then. It closes after as many of Jupiter's orbits as the ratio counts (`gap::closes_in`), not one.
- **The two groups are told apart in the shader by a and e** (`ride::bounds`, the shader's `uGroups`), in the file's floats; the crate's test reads the Trojans against every row's class. Never type a bound in `scene.ts`.
- **The Hildas and the Trojans are drawn apart, from places the crate integrates** (`integrate.rs`; `scene.ts`'s `integrate`, `uIntegrated`): the belt's shader turns their rows away by the same a and e the crate gathers them by (`ride::group`, `Elements::grouped`), so change the groups' bounds in `ride.rs` alone. Everything but a dot's place is the belt's shader and uniforms; a change to how a dot is drawn reaches both.
- **The places' address holds; a view over it doesn't.** `scene_integrated` is asked with the frame's other questions and returns an address; the view is made in `render`, at the upload, with no call into the wasm between.
- **The ride's names and triangle are placed on the crate's three points** (`ride::points`), asked for when the years move while the frame is turned. A name's offset from its point is `scene.ts`'s (`RIDE_NAMES`), and `see.ts` holds a copy.
- **The camera's flight to the ride is between two ends that hold still** (`scene.ts`, `anchor`, `since`): the camera above is taken at Jupiter's longitude in the last frame drawn at rest, and Jupiter's turn since is summed a frame at a time and added on. Never fly toward the camera above as it is now: the shortest way to a moving end changes sides, and the camera jumps.
- **The groups' light and the page's sentence about it come in together,** over the second half of the camera's flight and only in space (`scene.ts`'s `uRide`, `host.rs`'s `shows`): change one with the other.
- **The ride's camera is a map's numbers in the frame that turns** (`scene.ts`, `rideView`, `rideHand`; `stats.riding`): x along Jupiter's line from the Sun, its ground turned back by Jupiter's longitude as the camera was placed (`anchor`). It opens as board 2 has it and is forgotten once the camera is back in the fixed frame (`rideOpens`); the fixed frame's is the visitor's, as it was left (`stats.camera`; `stats.distance` is the camera's now, whichever it is). Its distance is kept as `rideZoom` times what the ride opens at, which follows the panel.
- **The opening shot is a pose: rolled 34°, the belt from the bottom left to the top right, and a hand gives the camera no roll** (`scene.ts`, `OPENING`; `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Input"). A pan, a zoom, and the keys keep it; the first drag with Shift eases it out to a level horizon in a third of a second (`camera.ts`, `POSE`, `level`), since a tilt and a turn are a level camera's; the way home gives it back, and the compass's needle leans with it. An arrow key goes across the window, not along the rolled plane (`nudge`). The ride's camera has none. A check that wants a level camera says `?roll=0`. `stats.gliding` is true while the camera moves on its own (the glide, a zoom's way, the flight home, the roll's ease): a check waits on it.
- **Shift is the key that tilts and turns; Ctrl is a pinch's, on a wheel alone** (`camera.ts`): a drag with Ctrl held pans.
- **The compass is told, never asked:** the scene calls `camera_seen` at the end of a frame it drew, when the camera's turn or tilt moved (by a tenth of a degree while it moves on its own, exactly once it rests) or a hand first moved a camera (`scene.ts`, `tell`). Only from its own frames: a call the crate made (a wheel, a drag) marks the frame to draw and returns. Its needle is the camera's bearing less the one it opened with, so home is up; riding, the ride's own.
- **An arrow key is a glide, a zoom key the double click's tween, and home a flight of the hand's own** (`camera.ts`, `nudge`, `zoomStep`, `home`): any press ends each, as it ends a glide, and `stats.gliding` is true through all three. The way home and the flight to a found row are one flight (`fly`), so a hand takes either over the same way; a flight in to a group, and back, is that too.
- **The scene is never called for a group: it asks** (`scene_pick`'s return each frame; when that moved, the shape, the years, the chip's text, and last the bytes' address: `scene.ts`, `regroup`). The bytes' address holds while the group does; with the group let go the scene's buffer keeps the old bytes while the gray goes out.
- **The outline and the chip are the scene's, in the names' layer, and the outline is written with no frame drawn** (`scene.ts`, `outline`, called from `drawStart`, `drawMove`, `drawEnd`): setting the scene dirty for each point draws every dot again. Round a group the outline is on the ecliptic (each point where its ray met it at the release) and shows only on the clock's years it was drawn on, in the fixed frame, in space.
- **The gray is the belt's shader's and the integrated rows' too** (`uGroup`, the attribute `picked`): the belt's is a byte buffer of the scene's own, the integrated rows' a copy picked out of it by row (`regroup`).
- **A group's flight in is the hand's own `fly`, toward the crate's box** (`group.rs`, `Group::fit`: the middle of where the group's dots were drawn, a hundredth left out each end; `scene.ts`, `regroup`, `flown`): through the camera kept at the shape's release (`shape.seen`), to the distance at which the box's sides on the ecliptic fill what the panel will leave (`scene_inset_to`, not the inset as it slides), found by halving with the camera placed and put back. Its time is the crate's (`host.rs`, `FLY_IN_MS`, the panel's slide too), which the scene asks for.
- **A group's flights run before a found row's in the scene's tick** (`flown`, then `chased`): a row found in the same frame has the camera.
- **A click's row is read off a pass of the scene's own** (`scene.ts`, `click`, `pickVertex`): the belt's buffers and the last frame's uniforms through another material, 13 px square, a row's depth its row. It shares the belt's and the integrated rows' geometries: an attribute or a rule that decides whether a row is drawn (no ellipse, drawn apart) changes there too. `host.rs` asks it at the release and takes it 200 ms later if no press, wheel, or key followed (`clicked`, `CLICK_WAITS`; `scene.rs`, `inputs`). The platform's own double click is two presses under 400 ms apart, so a slow one's second press can come after the pick, and takes it back (`undo_click`). With a drawn group held a click finds a row of the group and picks nothing else (`pick_row`).
- **The scene is never called for a find: it asks** (`scene_found`, `scene_finds`, each frame; `found_place` when the row or the clock moved, or the two groups went on or off their integrated places: it answers where the scene draws the dot, never `place_of`; `found_name` when the row did). The camera flies when the count of finds moves, so a row found while the dots are on the axes, or riding, waits (`scene.ts`, `chase`: `waiting`) until the camera is the fixed frame's at rest. `stats.chase` is a frame behind the crate: a check waits on `stats.finds` too (`see.ts`, `chaseIs`).
- **The flight to a row is the hand's own `fly`, and following begins only where it lands** (`camera.ts`, `fly`'s `landed`; `scene.ts`, `chased`, `follow`): a hand that takes the flight leaves the camera where it is, not following. Following, the scene sets what the camera looks at every tick, after the hand's step and before the draw, so a wheel and a drag with Shift go round the row; a pan's first move, an arrow, and the way home end it (`dragMove`, `nudge`, `home`), and the row stays marked.
- **A row is off the ecliptic and the camera looks at a point on it** (`camera.ts`, `aimedAt`): the point where the line of sight through the row meets the plane, so the row is at the picture's middle whatever the tilt. The camera's distance is from that point, not from the row (`under` apart): following holds it past `under`, or the row would be behind the eye.
- **The found row's mark is in the names' layer** (`scene.ts`, `found`), so it leaves with space by `leave`'s one number; its name is the last of `scene.labels()`, and a group's chip the one before it.
- **A picked gap's path breaks where it passes behind the eye** (`scene.ts`, `mark`): a place with no place on screen is a NaN in `pathDrawn`, and the line is drawn in runs. Anything else projected by hand through the camera's matrix needs the same test (`onScreen` has it).
- **The camera's numbers are `camera.ts`'s `View`, and `scene.ts`'s `place` is the only reader that makes a camera of them.** A pan and a zoom lean on one fact, that the ray through a window position meets the ecliptic where `ground` says through the camera as placed now: change how the camera is placed (the view offset beside the panel, the roll) and both still hold, but move the camera anywhere else and they don't.
- **Near the horizon a pixel is tens of au:** a pointer holds a point of the ecliptic only within `REACH` camera distances along the plane. Past it a drag holds the point at the reach, and a wheel zooms toward what the camera looks at, as over the sky. What the camera looks at stays within `AT_MOST` of the Sun.
- **A glide's speed is read from the page clock when the crate hands each move over,** not from the events' own times, which GPUI doesn't pass on: moves that arrive bunched after a long frame read as faster than the hand was.
- **What isn't a row leaves the picture by one number** (`flight::space`, the scene's `leave`): the sky, the Sun, Jupiter, its orbit, the names, the haze, and the page's line about the stars. Anything else the scene adds to space joins `leave`, or it stays over the axes.
- **The haze is off on the axes** (`look.ts`, `setHaze`): it lights the gaps (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Look").
- **The Sun, Jupiter, and its orbit's line are drawn in that order by `renderOrder`:** all three are transparent, which Three.js sorts far to near, so without it the line crosses Jupiter's disc on the far half of its orbit.
- **A name's size is measured when it's made and when a font arrives, never in a frame** (`scene.ts`, `measure`): a read of `offsetWidth` in the draw is a layout every frame.
- **Every shader of the scene writes linear light, into the look's buffer; only the look's last pass writes the display's values** (its tone map, then `#include <colorspace_fragment>`). A shader that draws to the canvas itself would need both; none does.
- **The look's haze is taken before the bodies are drawn** (the Sun, Jupiter, the lines), so only rows and stars glow; keep that order (`look.ts`).
- **A dot's size and its opacity both follow the camera's nearness** (`scene.ts`, `render`); change one without the other and a near view fades out or burns white.
- **H alone is the wrong brightness, and a row with none is NaN:** the shader divides a row's flux by the squares of its distance from the Sun and from the eye, and finds a NaN by the float's bits, never by a comparison (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Look").
- **The Sun's glow is a square that faces the camera, not a point:** a point is dropped whole when its center leaves the window, and is only as big as the GPU allows (`scene.ts`, `SUN_VERTEX`). Anything else drawn wide goes the same way.
- **A GPU that can't draw into a half-float buffer gets 8 bits and no bloom** (`look.ts`): every dot is still drawn, nothing is brighter than white. Not seen on a real device; no check covers it.
- **The ramp is the crate's** (`ramp.rs`): the shader's stops and the legend's slices come from the one table. Never type a tint in `scene.ts`.
- **Rows drawn alone (`?only`) skip the haze, the bloom, the sky, and the bodies,** so a disc found in a screenshot is the disc: `see.ts`'s place check leans on it.
- **The sky's file is the page's, not the crate's:** fetched whole after the rows file and the belt, never waited on, and a failure is the console's alone. The page's line about the stars shows only once they're drawn (`sky_drawn`).
- **A lost WebGL context isn't handled:** the belt's buffers are gone with it, and nothing fills them again (`PLAN.md`, open questions).
- **The data's URL is under the app's base** (`import.meta.env.BASE_URL` in `main.ts`, the same base in `vite.config.ts`'s plugin): never write `/data/…` from the origin's root, which on the shared hostname is Tycho's.
- **Until the scene draws, what shows under the interface is the page's own background** (`index.html`), the color the look's floor tints the scene with (`scene.ts`'s `PAGE`): keep the two the same.
- **Four of the crate's gotchas have a half here** (`crate/CLAUDE.md`): `RIDE_LIGHT` changes with `host.rs`'s `RIDE_LINE` ("The sky leaves by a number of its own"); `scene_pick` is never called from a call the crate made ("The pick is the crate's"); `alpha.ts` is one of the two things that make GPUI's canvas transparent ("GPUI's canvas is transparent"); and `scene.ts`'s `glass` agrees with `host.rs`'s `GLASS` ("The panel is glass in two places").

## Commands

From the repo root.

```
just kirkwood dev      # debug wasm + the Vite dev server with COOP/COEP; the three files from data/ (?date=YYYY-MM-DD for where the clock starts, ?paused to hold it; ?flight=<0 to 1> to hold the sort flight partway, ?ride=<0 to 1> the ride with Jupiter, and with ?ride=1, ?gap=<0 to 3> a gap held picked (the 3:1, 5:2, 7:3, 2:1); ?integrate=0 to leave the Hildas and the Trojans on their ellipses; ?perf for the table's probes, with ?probe-cells its drawn cells; ?live=<a second> for the live columns' rate and ?liveDecimals=<0 to 3> for a moving cell's decimals, both for looking; the scene's knobs, above)
just kirkwood build    # release wasm alone (-p) + wasm-opt, Vite build, asset sizes
just kirkwood preview  # serve that build, and the three files
```
