Raw: `2026-10-09-m7-cells-ab.json` (the TTFP A/B), `2026-10-09-m7-cells.json` (`just tycho cells`), `2026-10-09-m7-cells-negative-control.json` (the same check on a build with the old cells), `2026-10-09-m7-cells-perf.json` (`just tycho perf --only table,jump`, reference + throttled, median of 10).

## What changed

Every value cell was a flex container. GPUI cuts text (`text_overflow`) only when the text node has a definite width, and a flex item's isn't, so no cell was ever cut: a right-aligned number wider than its column spilled out the cell's left side, and the cell's clip took its front (`45.12345678901234` could read `5.12345678901234`). Text cells spilled right, with no "…".

- Cells are plain blocks (line height = row height), so GPUI's `.truncate()` works. Text ends in "…".
- Doubles round to the digits that fit (`format::fit_float`): the exact shortest text if it fits, else rounded in plain notation, scientific only when the whole part doesn't fit or plain keeps fewer than 3 significant digits. The budget is the column's width over the widest advance of `0-9 . - + e`, read from the font once.
- Integers are never rounded. Integer columns fit their type's widest value (BIGINT: 20 characters). Anything wider is cut in the middle (GPUI's `TruncateMiddle`); no current integer source reaches that (Parquet has no 128-bit integer: DuckDB writes HUGEINT as DOUBLE). DECIMAL is cut in the middle too (`9999999…999`).
- Text with line breaks shows them as `¶` (a cell is one line; `↵` isn't in IBM Plex Sans), and its tooltip has the real ones.
- One tooltip, for the hovered cell: the pointer maps to row and column from the scroll model (no per-cell hitbox), and after 500 ms (and once its page is loaded) a rounded, cut, or one-lined value shows its exact text (gpui-kit's `Tooltip` view with its content wrapped at 480 px and `shadow_lg` over its default `shadow_md`, gpui-base's `TooltipPositioner`, in a `deferred`). A cut is found by shaping that one cell's text. Within 300 ms of a tooltip going, the next shows at once. A scroll, a resize that moves the cells, a press, or leaving the cells hides it.
