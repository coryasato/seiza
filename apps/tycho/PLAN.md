# PLAN.md — Tycho

What's next for Tycho: milestone status, the next milestone's "done when" checks, the roadmap after it in order, and open questions. Only what's still open lives here. Context and rules are in `CLAUDE.md` here and at the repo root.

**Goal:** a data workbench for files too big for a spreadsheet, running entirely in the browser: open a file, understand it, check what you need, and export the part you want. The data never leaves the visitor's machine. Tycho is a utility app (root `CLAUDE.md`, "Tiers"): a public tool, built around a *controller* that sits between DuckDB-Wasm and the tall table, holds one *view state*, and can be driven by a person or by an outside LLM. Tycho hosts no model and takes no API key.

**The test for every feature:** does it help someone with a large file and a question? The first release is built for the quick checker, and takes the ML engineer's JSONL with it ([`docs/personas.md`](docs/personas.md); `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scope", 2026-11-25).

**Tycho is being rebuilt,** locally: production stays as deployed (tag `tycho-v1`) until "Ship the rebuild", the first release's last item.

**Where everything else lives:**

- [`../../docs/research/tycho-sample.md`](../../docs/research/tycho-sample.md): the sample: why GCAT, its columns, and the spec its build starts from.

## M15: sort, filter, and find

Opened 2026-11-26 from the first release's first item, and planned in five parts, one session each. It is local, as every item before "Ship the rebuild" is.

**What it is:** the rows in view chosen and ordered by the view state, and every row searched: the DuckDB source's order, its `WHERE`, and its find, which are the engine's unbuilt half; the view state's filters and sort, compiled to SQL and to the table's calls; the grammar's `filter`, `sort`, `clear`, and `find`; and the DOM they open, the toolbar's controls, the sort's and the filter's editors, the menus' sort, filter, keep, and leave-out items, and find's bar (the research's "The controller" and "The table's own tools"). Its form is the design session's (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scope", 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-21: one toolbar control a kind, never a chip a filter; the row count beside the chips, find at the toolbar's right; a column's menu, a cell's menu, and the editors; Ctrl+F; the page's keys; a command is a verb, then a column, then its parts; a pasted action waits for Enter) and the boards on [the design canvas](https://claude.ai/artifact/CR8skvUDrUsVxE4zXJtRmK): the "Toolbar" page (Toolbar 0 and 1, Option 1 with its list open, Option 3 and the icons at a phone, Find inline, Find at a phone and just opened); the "Columns and filters" page's Columns 3 (the sort chip, open), Headers A's sort arrow, and Filter 1 to 3; the menus' sort and filter items on Columns 1 and 2; and Phone 5's line under the toolbar. A gap between a board and the build is specced and decided, never silent.

**Decided at its opening** (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scope", 2026-11-26): the table's `sort` and `find` are turned on, the table's, the bridge's, and the engine's adapter's (`seiza-duckdb` gains `sort` and `find`, each turning on the table's, since the delegate's calls exist only under them), and no other feature (`replace_rows` is the base's); the filter editor is built with its fields and its foot, and the profile's preview in it (Filter 1's and 3's bars, Filter 2's checklist and counts) comes with the profile, `is any of` a typed list until then; find counts its matches with a query of Tycho's own, as Find inline's "3 of 18" draws it.

**Not in it:** the profile and its preview in the editors, the header strip, and grouping ("Profiles and the catalog"); a sort by several columns (after the first release); `OR` between filters (none in the first release); a filter control drawn in a header (not asked of the table); `sql` and the view copied as SQL ("Export"); a link that holds a view.

### Part A: the engine's half, and the view state's filters and sort, in Rust

- [x] **A1. How a filtered or sorted view pages, chosen by its timings.** The research's three ways ([`tall-table-features.md`](../../docs/research/tall-table-features.md), "`sort`"), now for a `WHERE` too: an index of row numbers written by one query (`CREATE TABLE … AS SELECT <row> … WHERE … ORDER BY …, <row>`) in an in-memory database the lockdown attaches, each page its slice of the index joined to the rows; a filtered or sorted copy; or a refusal past the memory budget, with its reason for the frame. Timed in DuckDB-Wasm, on the release build, on GCAT as the sample (Parquet) and as a CSV, and on `drop/asteroids-x3.parquet` and `drop/asteroids-x27.parquet` (`just tycho drop-files`, about 100 MB and 1 GB; the x27 with the default 30,720-row groups, not its `-rg` variants): the time to the first filled frame after a sort and after a filter, a page's read after it, and DuckDB's memory. Tycho keeps no results file: the timings go in the decision that picks the way, which says what a file too large is answered with. Also decided here, from the same runs: what a sort or a filter does while a CSV is still loading. An index or a copy goes in a database attached with `(COMPRESS)`, and its row count is checked against the count it was built from: past about 2.1 GiB of in-memory tables `CREATE TABLE … AS` stores nothing and says nothing (`engines/duckdb/CLAUDE.md`), so an empty or short one is a refusal, never "no rows". *Done 2026-11-26:* timed in DuckDB-Wasm on the release build through `?bench`'s bridge, every way and its pages at the top, middle, and 90%, with DuckDB's memory (`engines/duckdb/docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Table source", 2026-11-26, holds every number). **A filter is an index** of row numbers in the file's order, a page its slice joined to the rows between the slice's first and last row number written into the SQL: built in 5–14 ms on GCAT, 156–860 ms at ×3, 1.3–8.3 s at ×27, 10–124 ms on the 178 MB CSV; pages 22–35 ms, 31–215 ms, 0.53–0.67 s (the file's own page cost; the same join without the bounds read every row group, 25 s a page). **A sort is a sorted copy** of the rows in view, paged by `rowid` in 2–8 ms: 0.3 s on GCAT, 7.5–8 s and ~145 MiB at ×3, 1.9–2.0 s on the CSV; a sort's index paged in ~2 s at ×3 and 25–33 s at ×27. **What's refused:** a Parquet file over 256 MiB (`table::SORT_LIMIT`: its copy reads the whole file, 21–25 s for a 1.1 M-row filter of ×27) draws no arrow; a CSV sort whose copy's estimate passes `csv::MEMORY_BUDGET`; and a CSV still loading is neither filtered nor sorted, said in the frame's line (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Table and paging", 2026-11-26). Every index and copy is checked against the count it was built from, and DuckDB's memory first. End to end, a header's arrow clicked (the release build, Chromium): GCAT filled 655 ms after the click, the 178 MB CSV 2.34 s, ×3 10.7 s with "Sorting by H…" meanwhile; ×27 draws no arrow; the CSV clicked while loading said why. A filter's end to end comes with the verbs (Part B): its index plus its first page, above.
- [x] **A2. The view state's filters and sort, compiled to SQL.** The view state gains `filters` (each a column by name, an operator, a value; combined with `AND`) and `sort` (a column and a direction, at most one), in `__tychoView` and the log's states, so `undo` and the way back restore them. The controller compiles them to the source's `WHERE` and `ORDER BY` (ties in the file's order, NULLs last both ways, as the table's `sort` asks), the new count to `replace_rows` (the view at the top), and a sort to the table's `set_sort`. The table tells its source the sort again after every `replace_rows`, so the source takes the new `WHERE` before `replace_rows` is called, the delegate's `sort` builds its order over the filter in effect, and the controller calls `set_sort` only when the sort changed (a filter alone is one rebuild, not two); the table's `Sorted` and `SortRefused` go into the view as its widths do. Every operator of the grammar for each column type, `contains` and `starts` ignoring case, `=` and `in` not; a value is written into SQL by one function that types and quotes it, never by joining the text the visitor or an agent gave (tested with quotes, backslashes, `%` and `_` in `contains`, and a NUL). The status bar's and Phone 5's count reads the rows in view of the file's ("29,118 of 71,431 rows"). *Done 2026-11-26:* `crate/src/filter.rs` (the operators by type, `literal`, `where_sql`, the sort's words), the view state's `filters` and `sort` (`crate/src/view.rs`), `Workbench::sync_rows` (the index built off the table, then `set_filter` and `replace_rows` in one update with the sort; a sort alone `set_sort`; what can't be built given back and said), the table's `Sorted` into the view and the log ("Sort by mass_kg, largest first"), `SortRefused` in the frame's line, and the status bar's "29,118 of 71,431 rows" (`frame::facts`). The engine's half is `seiza_duckdb::view::ViewRows` over Tycho's `Source`, a `RowSource` (the CSV's chunks numbered across the file, a range reading only its chunks). Native tests: every operator for each type, the quoting (quotes, backslashes, `%` and `_`, a NUL refused, a date that's SQL refused), a state refused whole, a sort and the way back through the controller in GPUI's test app. The SQL checked against native DuckDB 1.4.5 on GCAT: a filter's pages equal to `LIMIT … OFFSET` over the same `WHERE`, the sorted copy's pages joining up in order both ways with NULLs last. A filter is reached in the browser only through Part B's verbs.
- [x] **A3. Find's source, and its count.** The delegate's `find` over the rows in view in their order, in windows, a new term cancelling it (the table's README, "Finding": the cell's text as `lower(CAST(… AS VARCHAR))`, hidden columns not searched); the count of matching cells and the current match's place among them in the order the steps follow (the shown columns in their drawn order, then down), each a query of their own, cancelled by a new term, so the bar can show "3 of 18". Timed on `drop/asteroids-x27.parquet` as A1's runs are, and what the bar shows while a count is out decided from them. *Done 2026-11-26:* the delegate's `find` over the rows in view in their order (`ViewRows`, windows from 16,384 rows doubling to 1,048,576, each its own query; a cancel stops it between windows and a page read waits at most one); `find_count_sql` and `find_place_sql` for the count and the current match's place, each a whole scan (checked against a scan in JS over native DuckDB on GCAT: 1,682 matches, the first match's place 1; the count's missing `sum` was found there). Timed on ×27: the first window ~0.58 s, 1 M rows ~2.3 s, 2 M ~4.1 s, a scan finding nothing ~87 s, the count 67 s; on GCAT a window 13–35 ms, the count ~40 ms. Decided: the bar says it's counting while a count is out, steps working, and shows the place and the count together (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scope", 2026-11-26). Sending them, and cancelling them on a new term, is the bar's (D1).
- [x] **A4. Native tests, and the size.** The compiled SQL of every operator and type, the quoting, a filter and a sort through `undo` and the way back in GPUI's test app, the table's own sort taken into the view, the paging over an index (or the way A1 picks) against a CSV's rows, and find's windows. `just tycho check`'s wasm size against the baseline, in the check's *Done* line as every part before has it: the table's rows read `sort` +23.4 KiB brotli and `find` +7.4 in the bare host, the engine's source, the SQL, and the grammar adding to them; within the size gate's 15%, or a row in `perf/budget-notes.md` that says why it's worth it. *Done 2026-11-26:* the native tests pass (Tycho 81, `seiza-duckdb` 30 with every feature: a filter's index and its count, a full DuckDB refused, a short index refused and dropped, a cancelled filter dropping its index, a filter's page between its bounds and an empty slice reading nothing, a page cancelled before its read, a sort's copy its pages wait for, a short copy failing its pages, a sort cancelling the one building, find's windows forward and backward, a view's tables named for its open and dropped when it closes). `just tycho check` passes: 2929.9 KiB brotli (limit 3283.0), +20.0 on M14's 2909.9, within the 15%. The code review's fixes: each open's view names its tables for the open (`f<n>_v<k>`), and closing a file drops its index and copy (a second file's sort collided with the first's names; checked on the release build, two files sorted twice each); the order listener holds its table weakly (it kept every table alive); a filter refused (DuckDB full, a CSV loading, a filter that no longer compiles, which no longer becomes `TRUE`) still gives the table the step's sort; a superseded or closed filter's task runs on to drop what it built; a step's log entry is written after the rows and the sort are given, and a way back they refuse isn't made current; a date checked against its month (`2024-02-31` refused), a fraction only after seconds; the first CSV chunk's memory recorded; `view.ts` fails at a header that never reads selected. ## Roadmap

Each item becomes a milestone when it's opened. Each links to its section of [the research](../../docs/research/tycho-workbench.md), which holds the reasoning and what's marked decided, proposed, or open; a proposed part is checked and reviewed when its item is opened, before it's built.

### The first release

Built locally; production stays as deployed until its last item. The order is decided in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scope", 2026-11-25.

1. **Sort, filter, and find,** with filter by a cell. Not thin: the DuckDB source's order, its `WHERE`, and its find are unbuilt engine work; the table's parts, `replace_rows` for filtering and `find`, are ready ("Waiting on"). *("The controller".)* *Opened as M15, above.*
2. **Profiles and the catalog,** grouping included, whose result is a new view. The profile comes in three stages, in the columns panel first. If it's too slow in use, a session of its own makes the sample faster before anything is cut; the header strip is asked of the tall table only after the panel's proves itself. *("The catalog"; "The column profile's cost", which holds the timings and that session's starting points.)*
3. **Export,** and the view copied as SQL. Its size plan is a cap (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scope", 2026-11-21); the cap is measured and the storage route tested first ("Export's cap", below). *("The table's own tools".)*
4. **Open from a URL:** Parquet only, read by range, on a route of its own. It changes what the first screen's input takes: a Parquet file's URL alone ("Paste a Parquet file's URL", as the Start boards draw it), anything else answered with an error, the command answers there now a stand-in it replaces; a URL pasted before React loads opens too (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scope", 2026-11-24). It comes before the connection policy, which has to know how this route loosens it. *("Opening a file".)*
5. **The instructions, and the clipboard and agent routes:** the Share menu's "Copy context", and the instructions an agent reads. *("How an LLM reaches it".)*
6. **Trust: the connection policy, offline, and the panel.** The policy is built against the URL route and the agent route as they stand, since it exists because the input is drivable. *("Trust".)*
7. **Views kept on the device, and the link to a view:** the views list's "Keep on this device" and "Forget", and the Share menu's "Copy link", as the design settled them (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scope", 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-21: "Views last for the session", "One Share menu", "A link to a view of a dropped file"). Where a kept view is stored is the policy's question, so it follows Trust.
8. **JSONL,** then nested values: the engine's half (how a `LIST`, `STRUCT`, or `MAP` becomes cells and short text) and their full form in the cell popover and the row detail view. A long form in the popover apart from the cell's text would be a new ask of the tall table (its `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Features", 2026-11-19). *("Opening a file".)*
9. **Ship the rebuild.** The new Tycho replaces the deployed one: the deploy; Gaia and the asteroids out of production's R2, which then holds DuckDB-Wasm's two wasm bundles alone; the zone's WAF rules and rate limit looked at again against the new build's requests, right after; and `README.md` and the root README's row as a visitor finds the app. Nothing of Kirkwood runs on Tycho's hostname: the zone's redirect for its old path answers before Tycho's rules and Worker. *The owner confirms every deletion in production and the deploy.* *(`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Hosting", 2026-11-23.)*

## Open questions

Named, not numbered: a question leaves when it's answered, its answer in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

- **The profile's speed.** How the engine keeps page reads ahead of a profile query, and whether a smarter sample is needed, tried before the graphic is cut (the build, then a session of its own). The engine's speed has had no measurement of its own since Tycho's were retired, so this is where its page reads and CSV loading are timed again if a number is needed.
- **Column names going out.** How column names, which are the file's text, go out in the copied context and the capability schema.
- **The policy and the URL route.** What DuckDB-Wasm needs the connection policy to allow, how the URL route loosens it, and where anything saved in the browser is stored, kept views first.
- **The lockdown in a browser.** A dropped file's handle is answered, with the engine unlocked until `LOAD parquet` succeeds (M12 part A). Left: a remote file in Chromium (a 416 on a URL the page had downloaded whole; "Open from a URL"), and an export handed to the page ("Export").
- **Export's cap.** The cap's size, measured; and whether a file can be written to the browser's own storage and saved from there, with the engine locked down, which would lift the cap (tested before export is built).
- **JSONL's size limit,** if it loads into memory as a CSV does, and what the engine's reader does with nested values today.
- **Dropped files on the URL route.** Whether the URL route takes them.
- **WebMCP and on-device models.** Where each stands in the browsers that ship today.
- **`undo` after a view opens.** What `undo` does after a command that opened a view (left by the design session for the build).
- **A kept view's size,** measured against the caps' estimate ("Views kept on the device").
