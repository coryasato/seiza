# Decisions: Observatory

Why the Observatory is built the way it is. Decisions that bind every app, the comparison protocol among them, are in the root [`docs/DECISIONS.md`](../../../docs/DECISIONS.md).

One bullet per decision, under its area, oldest first: what was chosen, the numbers that chose it, and *[where they came from]* (a milestone, a code review, a results file in `perf/results/`). A decision that replaces another marks the old one *Superseded* rather than deleting it.

## Data

- **2026-10-14** · **The data file: Tycho's asteroids, 11 of 18 columns, all 1,567,523 rows,** 21.76 MiB (Tycho's is 35.9 MiB, over the 25 MiB static-asset limit). Kept: one of each kind the table draws (spkid; full_name and class; neo and pha; H, diameter, albedo, a, e; first_obs), the readable name over the designations, and the two mostly-NULL floats, so both sides draw empty cells. Dropped for size: pdes, name, i, q, per_y, last_obs, n_obs_used. Tycho's writer settings (ZSTD, 30,720-row groups), so a page costs the reads Tycho's does. Built from Tycho's file, checked against its pinned SHA-256; rebuilds are byte-identical. *[M0 part B, `perf/results/2026-10-14-m0.md`]*

## Hosting

- **2026-10-14** · **Everything under `/observatory/`.** The page's Vite `base` is `/observatory/` from the first commit, so dev, preview, and production share paths, and the engine's extensions are served from `/observatory/duckdb/` (`@seiza/duckdb`'s `extensionDir`, added for it; `engines/duckdb/docs/DECISIONS.md`). Tycho keeps `/duckdb-ext`: its app wasm byte-identical, its engine chunk +206 B raw (+187 B brotli), `just tycho engine` passing. `shared-web`'s brotli preview strips the base as well, so preview serves the build as production will. *[M0 part A and its code review, `apps/tycho/perf/results/2026-10-14-observatory-m0-a-engine-review.json`]*
- **2026-10-14** · **The apex `seiza.io` is this Worker's custom domain** (wrangler made its DNS record; there was none). Everything outside `/observatory/` is a 404 until the landing page is decided (`PLAN.md`, open questions). *[M0 part B deploy]*
- **2026-10-14** · **The data is a static asset, served by range through a Worker and the edge cache.** Static assets ignore `Range` (200 and the whole file, MISS every time, whatever the headers, extension, or type), so DuckDB-Wasm can't read them. The Worker runs first for `/observatory/data/*` only: it puts the whole file into `caches.default` once per location, keyed by its SHA-256 (`DATA_SHA256`, set at deploy), and answers every request with `cache.match`, which serves ranges from it. First page 390.4 ms against Tycho's R2 path's 474.9 (median of 10, edge warm); the first request at a location under a new key 2.27 s (n = 1). Each read is a billed Worker request (the paid plan). Rejected: R2 as Tycho does it (proven, but R2 reads on misses and the pause switch back in play); slicing the static asset's stream per request (up to 22.8 MB through the Worker for each read). *[M0 part B, `perf/results/2026-10-14-m0.md`]*

## Scroll model

- **2026-10-15** · **The TanStack contender's scroller: a 2^24 px spacer, re-anchored with `scrollBy`.** One spacer height for every browser, 16,777,216 px: under Firefox's 17,895,696 px, past which Firefox drops an element's height to 0 (Chromium and WebKit clamp at 33,554,428). Wheel, keys, touch, and any step of at most a screen move the content 1:1 (V = s + D); past 1,800 px of drift from the canonical map, or pinned at an end, the scroller is re-anchored in the same task, which no frame showed at a wheel's pace in any of the three browsers. `scrollBy`, not a `scrollTop` assignment: the assignment lost Page Down input in Firefox (19,884 of 26,040 px) and fling distance in WebKit (a steady fling 260,503 px short where `scrollBy` reached the end). Jumps go through `scrollBy` too. The canonical map is 1:1 within 2^20 px of each end, so a steady fling (180 equal events) and a wheel from near the end both reach the last row exactly. Design: `dom-scroll-model.md`. Rejected: a spacer at each browser's own limit (a stretch that differs per browser for no gain: the stretch only scales a thumb drag); re-anchoring only at rest (a fling would run the scroller into its end). *[M1 part A, `perf/results/2026-10-15-m1-a.md`]*
- **2026-10-15** · **A thumb drag is told apart by its step, not by pointer events.** A step bigger than a screen with no wheel, key, or touch in the last 300 ms is the thumb (one pixel of thumb travel is ~19,000 px of scroller), mapped through the canonical map; the 80 ms drag settle is 80 ms without a scroll event. Chromium, WebKit, and Firefox (headed) also send `pointerdown`/`pointerup` for a scrollbar press (no `pointermove` during a drag in Chromium and WebKit), and any scroll while the pointer is down on the scrollbar is the thumb. *[M1 part A]*
- **2026-10-15** · **TanStack Virtual over a moving window of 4,096 rows, not the full count.** The full count held 29.91 MiB more (its `Float64Array` of starts and sizes) and built in a 28.4 ms task at mount (mount to rows 20.7 against 8.3 ms), for the same fling work (4.22 against 4.20 ms of main thread per frame, median of 10, Chromium at DPR 2). The subscriber forces a render when the window's or the content's base moves, since TanStack skips one when its index range is unchanged; without it, 336 of 374 fling frames showed the wrong rows. `@tanstack/react-virtual` pinned at 3.14.19 (`virtual-core` 3.19.4). *[M1 part A, `perf/results/2026-10-15-m1-a.md`]*
- **2026-10-15** · **The contender handles its keys.** ↑/↓ a row, a screen for Page Up/Down and Space (an instant `scrollBy`, so still on the compositor), the ends for Home/End with or without Ctrl and for Cmd+↑/↓, all through the model, as the component does; 5 ↓ moved 5 rows in all three browsers. Native arrows move 40 px in Chromium, WebKit under Playwright doesn't arrow-scroll a focused element, and the browser's Ctrl+End runs the scroller, not the content, to its end. *[M1 part A]*


## Measurement

- **2026-10-15** · **Input to visible response comes from Chromium's trace, `EventLatency`, for every input kind on both sides, each reading guarded on every run.** It reports GPUI's prevented wheel (`MOUSE_WHEEL`) as well as keys and presses, and named the probe's frame (the first drawn at the new position) in 180 of 180 GPUI inputs on WebGPU and WebGL2. On the DOM's native scrolls the compositor moves the content before the main thread knows: the wheel's `EventLatency` (`FIRST_GESTURE_SCROLL_UPDATE`) ended 23–30 ms before the main-thread probe in 30 of 30, and a session's first track press ended 19 ms before it (2 of 30); in all 60 it ended on exactly the frame whose compositor first applied the scroll (`Scroll Delta This Frame`). A control scrolling the same page from a main-thread listener put `EventLatency` back on the probe's frame in 30 of 30. So the guards: GPUI's reading must match the probe; a DOM main-thread move (the contender's keys) the probe; a DOM native scroll the compositor's frame. A cold run measures a first press, where the main-thread probe is late. Rejected as the source: Event Timing as a page reads it (rounded to 8 ms, nothing under 16 ms: 3 of the DOM's 60 keys and presses missed; no wheel); the trace's `EventTiming` equals `EventLatency` for keys and presses and adds nothing. The page's live readouts may use Event Timing for keys and presses, labelled. *[M1 part B, `perf/results/2026-10-15-m1-b.md`]*
- **2026-10-15** · **Per-frame work is each frame's `ProxyMain::BeginMainFrame` in the trace, on both sides, itemized, and the main thread's whole busy time beside it.** The frame covers the whole rendering update, split into input dispatched in it, animate (rAF callbacks and scroll events), lifecycle (style, layout, paint), and the rest (commit); the DOM's work is mostly lifecycle (2.64 of ~3.7 ms a frame), which `workRecorder` (rAF callbacks that draw) can't see. As an instrument it holds: on Tycho's fling its rAF part matched `workRecorder` to 0.02 ms per frame (p50, 10 cold runs, spread 0.07 ms); the whole frame adds 0.41 ms (input 0.16, the update after the callbacks 0.24). ~8% of busy time runs outside any frame on both sides (GPUI: the engine's page results, read in wasm in the message's task, and timers), so main-thread CPU cost is the busy total, not frames × per-frame work. Tycho's work per frame (`workRecorder`, the panel) is the rAF part only, ~0.4 ms less, and doesn't compare with these numbers. *[M1 part B]*
- **2026-10-15** · **The contender contract:** marks (`contender:first-pixels`, `usable`, `open`, `filled`), the state object `__contender.state` with `?measure`, the targets, and the replay hooks, in `contender-contract.md`. Contenders publish; one metrics module reads. *[M1 part B]*
