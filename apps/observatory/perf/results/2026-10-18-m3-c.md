## What changed

- **`scroll.ts`** (the scroll model): Page Down/Up and Space/Shift+Space move a screen less a row; a track press jumps (the thumb centered under the press, in the press's own task), with the grab after it where the browser allows it and the browser's later paging ignored while the press is held; a scroll in thumb mode is a drag (`onDrag`), ended by a release, the paging's 80 ms drag settle with no scroll, or a wheel, key, or touch; Shift turns a vertical wheel sideways through a cancelable listener that's on only while Shift is down. `docs/dom-scroll-model.md` has the model as built.
- **`tooltip.ts`** (new): the cells' tooltip, the tall table's `hover_at`/`hide_tooltip`/`decide_tooltip` in DOM terms, placed by gpui-base's `Positioner` rules, styled as gpui-kit's `Tooltip` (`view.css`). `cells.ts`'s `cellTooltip` gives its text (the exact text, capped at 2,000 characters, and whether the cell already differs); a cut is the drawn cell's `scrollWidth` over its `clientWidth`.
- **`view.ts`**: the model's drags call `TablePaging.startDrag`/`endDrag` (whose `dragSettleMs` the model reads); the tooltip, refreshed after each render (a page landing under a rested pointer); a press outside the table keeps its focus; `?probe` publishes the paging and the model as `__tanstackProbe` for the check, never in a measured run.
- **`perf/tanstack-parity.ts`** (`just observatory tanstack-parity`, in `check`): the checks below; `test/view.test.ts` adds the tooltip's text.
- **Sizes (brotli):** the TanStack page's first-paint JS 68.8 → 70.2 KiB, its CSS 0.9 → 1.0 KiB; the after-paint chunks unchanged (data layer 1.8 + 1.0, Arrow 31.9, engine 7.5). The GPUI contender: unchanged (2,697.3 KiB wasm). No other app builds either.

## Where the DOM side still differs

- **The grab after a track press.** The tall table drags the thumb it jumped while the press is held. The DOM side jumps in every browser, but the drag after it is the browser's: Chromium grabs the thumb with classic scrollbars (checked in the experiment: 80 px of pointer moved 286,502 px of scroller) but not with macOS's overlay ones; WebKit doesn't; Firefox sends the page its moves, and the model drags. Where it doesn't, the content stays at the jump. A press on the thumb itself is the browser's own drag, the same everywhere. Closing it would mean a scrollbar of the page's own (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, rejected).
- **A press's landing row.** The DOM thumb maps through the scroll model's canonical map, 1:1 near the ends, so a press at 30% of the track lands at 27.5% of the rows, where GPUI's lands at 30%. At 50% both land at 50%.
- **Not in this part's list:** the jump-to-row highlight (the contract's `replay.jump`, part D) and the middle cut of an integer or a decimal (this file's integers always fit; the DOM cuts at the end).

## What surprised us

- **No browser lets the page cancel a track press's paging,** and pointer capture brings no moves during a scrollbar press in Chromium or WebKit. Moving the scroller in the press's own `pointerdown` does work: with the thumb already under the pointer, Chromium and Firefox have nothing to page toward.
- **WebKit pages twice after an exact jump** (it decided to page at the press), and the press going idle cleared the guard while the pointer was still down; the guard now holds until the release and the rest after it.
- **macOS's overlay scrollbars take a press only while shown**; a press there on a hidden one reaches the cells (its target is the cell, not the scroller), which is how the model tells them apart.
- **WebKit can't let the page cancel a wheel in the frame Shift went down:** its scrolling hasn't seen the new listener. The listener now leaves an uncancelable wheel to the browser rather than scrolling both ways.
- **Firefox warns of a "scroll-linked positioning effect"** on the scroll model (content placed from a scroll listener): its design, not a fault; the check ignores that warning with the engine's.
- **The data file never rounds a float** at its 120 px columns (the longest is 8 characters); its tooltips are all cut names (4,314 names of 30+ characters).

## Experiments

One-off scripts on a plain scroller (a 2,000,000 px spacer, 800×600), with styled classic scrollbars and with the contender's `scrollbar-color` ones, recorded here:
- `preventDefault` on `pointerdown` or `mousedown` for a track press: the press still paged, in all three browsers.
- A jump in `pointerdown` to put the thumb under the press: then held 400 ms and moved 50 px down: Chromium (classic) no paging and a grab (the scroller followed the pointer), WebKit two pages of paging and no grab, Firefox (headed) no paging, no grab, and `pointermove`s delivered to the page. With overlay scrollbars, Chromium paged a little and didn't grab.
- `setPointerCapture` in that `pointerdown`: no `pointermove` during the press in Chromium or WebKit.
- The thumb's drawn length at a 2,000,000 px scroller: 22–26 px in all three (screenshots), so the 24 px estimate.
- A press at the right edge on a hidden overlay scrollbar: target the content, no paging; on a shown one: target the scroller, paged.

## After the code review

A review (medium) found, and this fixes:
- **A classic scrollbar's arrow buttons** (Windows) were taken for the track: the down arrow jumped to the table's end. A press within the bar's width of either end is now the browser's. Not checked here: macOS's scrollbars have no buttons.
- **In a scroller under ~240 px** (a split or embedded view), Chromium's page step (87.5% of the scroller) is more than the rows' viewport, so its paging after a jump would have moved the content. The guard now takes steps up to the scroller's height. Not checked either: at 260 and 120 px pages no browser paged that far after a jump with the old guard (a control run passed), and at 120 px the track is too short for a press off the thumb at 30%.
- **The status line couldn't be selected:** the page's press guard (keeping the table's focus) now applies only while the table is open.
- **The tooltip's refresh ran after every render,** a fling's forced ones included; now only after a render a page landing caused.
- **A cut by less than a pixel** showed an ellipsis but no tooltip (`scrollWidth` rounds): the cut is now the text's own width (a `Range`) against the room inside the cell's padding. The cut-name checks pass on it.
- **Shift pressed while another frame had the keys** never turned the wheel sideways; the passive wheel listener now sees it, and the next wheel goes sideways.
- **The check's overlay-scrollbar press was flaky:** a held press in Chromium once landed on the cells (the bar had faded); `downOnBar` now checks that the scrollbar took the press (its target the scroller) and presses again if not.
- The check imports the row height, the drag settle, and the tooltip's delay and grace from the contender instead of copying them.

Kept: a drag right after a jump, in a table short enough for a thumb pixel to be less than a screen of scroller, would be taken for paging (not this table: a thumb pixel is ~9,500 px); and the model's own 80 ms drag settle beside the paging's, which ends a drag whose release never came (both read the paging's one value). `2026-10-18-m3-c-parity.json` is the run after the fixes; the first-paint JS is 70.5 KiB brotli.
