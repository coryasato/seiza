# M9 part E3b: the canvas's heat (2026-11-04)

Measured on an Apple M1, Darwin 25.6.0 arm64, machine `local`, Chromium 153.0.8010.12 headless=new, 1440×900 at DPR 2, reference only (root CLAUDE.md, "Measurement protocol"). Load average ~2.2 → ~4.6 over the session (the user's machine in use).

Raw: `2026-11-04-m9-e3b-spot-wide-sheet-rows-20.json`, `2026-11-04-m9-e3b-spot-wide-sheet-rows-18.json` (the spot check, 3 runs a side, on the final builds). The A/Bs below were run unlabeled; their numbers are copied here.

## What changed

- The wide sheet's heat is M8's canvas's: a diverging ramp, cold `oklch(0.6 0.13 262)` / middle `oklch(0.985 0 0)` / warm `oklch(0.64 0.18 32)` in the light theme, `oklch(0.48 0.12 262)` / `oklch(0.2 0 0)` / `oklch(0.55 0.17 32)` in the dark, over −35 … 12 … 40 °C, clamped, mixed continuously in Oklab (not the canvas's 11 sketch steps: decided with the user). The ramp had been the theme's background to `chart-1` through the file's range (−75.5 … 51.1).
- One home: `contenders/scenarios.ts`'s `WIDE_SHEET.heat`. The TanStack side converts the stops to hex once and sets them as root variables per theme (`scene.ts`, `heatCss`). The GPUI side's are constants in `scene.rs`, which `perf/wide.ts` checks against `scenarios.ts`. The domain reaches GPUI as the scene's numbers (`mid` added, 8 numbers). `__RANGES__` and `chart-1` (in `theme.css`) are gone.
- The page's drawings use the same stops and domain (`gallery.ts`, `page.css`). Its rows and days are now the canvas's: latitudes 70 → −62 and 24 days, one in 15.
- The wide sheet's description gains the canvas's line "The seasons show as a warm band along each row; scrolling south, the band flips."
- Sizes: the GPUI wasm wasn't sized (`check` sizes it at the close). The page's first-paint JS went 84.2 → 84.3 KiB brotli (≤ 86.0).

## Experiments and controls

**The first TanStack version cost frames.** It set each stop as `light-dark(oklch(…), oklch(…))` on the root. The first spot check put TanStack at ~940 down at 41.0, 35.0, 33.9 % (part D: 47.1–50.4), and a repeat at 37.7–40.5. An interleaved A/B, TanStack's wide build at HEAD (two hex variables) against it, 4 rounds of 2 runs, ~940, reference, load ~4:

**Controls:** the two-stop ramp against the drawn pixels (0 of 720 matched). The hex conversion against GPUI's float sRGB: both within 2 steps of the script's ramp at every sampled cell.

**After the code review:** GPUI's stops are now converted once per theme, where they had been converted per column per frame. A GPUI spot check at ~940 after it, 3 runs, load ~4.6: down 99.2, 100, 100 % (work p50 14.1 ms, 14.05 before); sideways 99.2 ×3; fast 98.4–99.2.

## The rest of the canvas

A pass over M8's canvas (`Main.dc.html`: the wide sheet's frame and route, the Overview, the TallTable page), against what's built:

- **The ramp's stops and domain:** the canvas's, as specced (its `--hc`, `--hm`, `--hw`; its index puts 12 °C at step 5, −35 and 40 at its ends). Continuous, not stepped (decided with the user).
- **Built here:** the drawing's rows and days (the canvas's 70 → −62, 24 days one in 15; were 70 → −40 and 20). The description's seasons line, which only the diverging ramp makes true.
- **Already decided, not gaps:**
  - The stops' lengths and labels (~380 … ~940, no heat-only stop: `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Scenarios", 2026-11-02).
  - The pinned columns, Station and Place (M9's spec; the canvas pins Station and Lat). The header "Latitude" (the file's column name).
  - The data credit's wording (NOAA's terms, "Scenarios", 2026-11-02).
  - The Overview's card as part E3's spec set it (the mosaic, the line, the rule). The canvas also draws variants with a keeps-up chart in the card; part E3 built the spec, not those.
- **Decided with the user:** the cells' negative numbers. The canvas writes them with a minus sign (U+2212, "−27.8"); both contenders show a hyphen-minus ("-27.8"), the table's own formatting, pinned by `cells.json` on both sides. Kept: a hyphen reads as a minus beside a temperature, and the minus would be an option asked of the table (rule 1).
