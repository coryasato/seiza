# M13 part A2: the monitoring wall's spike on both sides, its stops, and its measure (2026-11-10)

Measured on Apple M1, Darwin 25.6.0 arm64, machine `local`, Chromium 153.0.8010.12 headless=new, 1440×900 at DPR 2, cold (a fresh browser per run), **3 runs** per side, stop, and profile (sizing the stops, not the protocol), reference and throttled (CPU 4×, Fast 4G). gpui-kit 0.9.1, React 19.3.1, TanStack Virtual 3.14.19 (each file's `versions`). Load average 3.4–5.3 over the runs (the machine in use). The throttle's busy loop read 115–119 ms at the reference and 480–490 ms throttled in every setting (4.1–4.2×), so every throttled time is real.

Raw, all from `spike/wall-run.ts`, measured after the code review's fixes (below):
- `2026-11-10-m13-a2-wall-review.json`: the sweep, 4, 6, and 8 tables × both sides × both profiles, 2k ticks a second a desk.
- `2026-11-10-m13-a2-wall-hold-gpui-review.json` and `-hold-tanstack-review.json`: Shown in's control at 8 tables, reference, each side free and a frame late.
- `2026-11-10-m13-a2-wall-drop.json`: the gap check's control, one batch dropped on each side; both runs fail it.

**Superseded:** `2026-11-10-m13-a2-wall.json`, `-wall-hold-gpui.json`, and `-wall-hold-tanstack.json`, the first runs. They were taken before the runs checked that every batch reached the side, so nothing in them proves no batch was lost. The rerun lost none, and agrees with them everywhere except throttled at 6 tables (below, where both are given).

## What changed

Nothing in the contenders. The spike (throwaway) has the two sides and the runner:

- **GPUI** (`spike/wall-gpui/`, crate `wall-spike`; `spike/wall/gpui.html`): `?tables=<n>` tall tables in one window, built with the wall's features (`custom-cells`, `live`, `format`, `density`), each a desk of 50 over one store. They're laid out in two rows of n / 2 on the sidebar's color, 8 px apart, each under a 26 px title. Columns, density, and paint are the canvas's: Symbol 64, Last 76, Chg % 72, Volume 80 px, 24 px rows, 12 px text; Last flashes for 400 ms at 30%, Chg % is drawn by its sign, Volume is grouped. Every batch is folded in its message's task, then each table's ticked rows are invalidated in runs. The probes are on, as the contender's are.
- **TanStack** (`spike/wall/dom.html`, `src/dom.ts`): `?tables=<n>` tables in one React tree, each with its own scroller and TanStack Virtual, laid out as above, with a gutter at the tall table's width for 50 rows at 12 px. The store is idiomatic (a new row object per tick, a new array per batch: A1's `objects`). It commits once a frame in the frame's callback (`flushSync`, as the contender's market level does), with rows memoized by their quote. The flash is the contender's: the cell's background, by keyframes.
- **The feed:** A1's worker over the shown desks' instruments (`wall/instruments.ts`), started once every table has filled (`wall:filled`), at 2k ticks a second a desk.
- **No engine:** a desk is one page, which the contender reads once and then answers from the store. Here every read is the store's.
- **Read the same way:** each batch's send, its generation per table, and the mask of tables whose on-screen rows it ticked. Each frame that drew any table's re-read is logged (`wall:refreshed`, right after it) with the generation drawn per table. A batch is paired with the first frame at or after its send at which every table in its mask has drawn its generation (A1-5's pooled pairing). From there it's `metric-feed`'s reading: keeps up, Shown in to the presentation, late frames, work, busy time, and collections (`metrics/metrics.ts`).
- **The same wall** (screenshots at 4 and 8 tables, both sides): the same desks, columns, formats, sign colors, and flashes. GPUI's tables show about 15½ rows to the DOM's 16 (its frame keeps room at the bottom). Every batch ticked an on-screen row on both sides (51–55 a second), so that half row changes nothing read here.

- **The fast device: both keep up at every stop,** with no late frames.
- **The slow device: the edges fall inside the dial.** TanStack keeps up at 4 tables and not at 6 (every one of its 6 runs at 77.6–85.0%). GPUI keeps up at 4, sits on the line at 6 (90.0% over 6 runs, 79.4–92.4), and doesn't at 8. The canvas's sketch ("GPUI keeps up at 8 tables, TanStack at 4") is a stop too generous to GPUI. GPUI's verdict at 6 may land either side of 90% in part D's 10 runs, and its spread there will be worth reading beside the median.
- **Pooling split nothing:** every batch's tables drew it in the same frame on both sides (`splitPct` 0, and Shown in to the first table's frame equals the pooled one in every run). One canvas draws every table in one frame, and so does one React root committing every table in one `flushSync`. So the wall measures N tables' drawing in one frame, on both sides, not whether they draw apart.
- **Shown in's send → mark grows with the tables on the slow device** (GPUI 22 → 31 → 37 ms, TanStack 12 → 17 → 25): the frames take longer than a refresh. GPUI's mark → presentation is about 1 ms (its mark follows the present) or about 10 ms, a frame scheduling state that differs between runs (`toPresentP50MsRuns`), as at market watch. TanStack's is 5–32 ms.

## Shown in's control: a frame late

At 8 tables, reference, 3 runs each. GPUI holds every batch 17 ms before folding it (`?hold`). TanStack commits each frame's snapshot of the store in the next frame (`?hold`).

| | Keeps up | Send → mark p50 | Shown in p50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPUI | 100% | 15.2 ms | 24.4 |
| GPUI, held 17 ms | 99.4% | **31.9** (+16.8) | 32.8 |
| TanStack | 100% | 9.8 | 23.6 |
| TanStack, a frame late | 99.8% | **26.9** (+17.1) | 42.1 |

The mark moves by what each variant holds, and keeps up doesn't move. The pairing across tables reads a side drawing late. Shown in moves less than the mark on GPUI here (+8.4), because its mark → presentation sat in its ~1 ms state in two held runs and its ~10 ms state in two free ones: the run's scheduling state, which `toPresentP50MsRuns` shows, as at market watch.

**The first TanStack control was wrong.** Its held commit rendered the store as it was at the commit, not the snapshot the frame had taken, so a batch was drawn by the next frame's held commit as usual and nothing was held (send → mark 10.4 against 10.0). It now commits the frame's snapshot. That run was discarded.

## After the code review

- **Every batch is checked whole before any of it is folded, on both sides,** as the contender's store does. A batch that isn't one is refused and logged (`wallFailures`, `__wall.failures()`). Before, GPUI's fold stopped at an out-of-range instrument with the ticks before it folded, and dropped the batch from the log in silence.
- **A run fails unless every batch the worker sent reached the side's log, in order** (its sequences 0, 1, 2, … with no gap), and fails on any batch refused or thrown. Its control, `--drop 30` (`?drop=`: one batch never reaches the side), fails both sides at batch 30. Every run of the rerun passed both checks.
- **`check-terms` reads the files git would track, not only those it does,** so new files are checked before they're added. The first spike files weren't checked at all. It also catches a part's ID alone ("A" and a number, with a check's "-n"), which the spike's comments and `market-run.ts` used. Both were reworded, as were two decisions' bodies.
- `?tables=` takes 4, 6, or 8 only, as its message says.

## Decided (with the user)

- **The stops stay 4, 6, and 8 tables at 2k ticks a second a desk.** The edges fall inside the dial on the slow device (TanStack between 4 and 6, GPUI between 6 and 8, its 6 on the line), so A1's fallback of 5k a desk isn't needed.
- **The verdict's measure is market watch's "keeps up" (≥ 90%), with A1-5's pooled pairing.** Pooled and per-table read the same here, since neither side draws its tables in separate frames. The split share stays a diagnostic in the runs.

## The suite's time

The sweep's 36 runs took 11 minutes with the build and 13.5 without it in the rerun, at a higher load: 18–23 s a run. Part D as planned:
- The feed at each stop: 3 stops × 2 sides × 2 devices × 10 runs, ~40 minutes.
- Usable, data, and memory once (market watch's), ~25 minutes.
- The gpui-kit 0.9.0 against 0.9.1 A/B (`metric response` and the wide sheet's `scroll` at one stop, reference, median of 10), ~20 minutes.

**About 1.5 h, one sitting.**
