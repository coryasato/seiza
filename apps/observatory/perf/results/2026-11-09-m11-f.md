# M11 part F: live tail shipped (2026-11-09)

Measured on Apple M1, Darwin 25.6.0 arm64, machine `local`, Chromium 153 headless=new, 1440×900 at DPR 2, unthrottled, one run per use (a peak, not a timing: `perf/burst.ts`). No new measurement of either contender: the README shows part D2's numbers (`2026-11-08-m11-d2.md`) through `report`.

Raw: `2026-11-09-m11-f-burst.json` (the requests one visitor sends in the busiest 10 s, nine uses, counted at the server).

## What changed

- **The README** has live tail's section: what the scenario is (NASA-HTTP's July 1995 log replayed, looped past its end, the clock one worker per frame), what Row shown in, keeps up, and a scroll back mean, where each side wins, where they're even, and where neither keeps up (100k on a slow device), and three generated tables: at each stop (keeps up as Row shown in p95, Row shown in p50, late frames, work per frame p95, time paused in collections, the scroll back, both devices), the runs' range (each side's fastest and slowest run's Row shown in p50 and scroll back at each stop), and once (usable, first pixels, memory once open and at 100,000 rows, the clock's first count to its frame, and the soak's memory and Row shown in p95 at its start and end). Its "Data credits" names NASA-HTTP (Jim Dumoulin, KSC; contributed by Martin Arlitt and Carey Williamson), the archive's terms, and what the file drops and cuts. The intro names the fourth scenario and the status bar's Shown in and follow toggle; the root README's apps table and summary name live tail.
- **`perf/budgets.json`:** live tail's 94 recorded metrics show in the README's three sections (`readme:live-tail-stops`, `-ranges`, `live-tail`), and two new ones, `live-tail-first-pixels-*` (the `usable` file's `firstPixelsMs`, as market watch's), as the footnote. The collection card reads time, as the page's does (part E4), so the README's stops table shows the time, not the count.
- **`burst` covers live tail:** `tail` (its route, each side filled at the default stop and run under the replay for 2 s, then the dial through the other three stops, the same at each) and `tail-reloads` (five reloads of its route). `dial` runs a feed's use for live tail as for market watch.

## Against the budgets

No budget changed. The builds are HEAD's (`builds`, rebuilt for this part, then again by the deploy); this part's changes are docs and a script (`perf/burst.ts`).

| market (both sides, every stop, under the feed) | 64 | 12 | 120 |
| market-reloads (market watch, 5) | 107 | 15 | 107 |

The peak is unchanged: 141, five reloads of the wide sheet's route, under the zone's per-IP limit (the limit and its margin: the gitignored `docs/private/`). Live tail is the busiest dial on the wire (76 in 10 s against the wide sheet's 66) but not the peak: each frame opens the log by range (a few reads), then fetches it whole once (one 9.7 MB request), and the replay reads every page from the registered file with no requests of its own.

## The deploy

`just observatory deploy` from this session (2026-11-09): the builds staged (70 files, 23.6 MiB on the wire as br; DuckDB's two oversize wasm as `.gz`), `wrangler deploy`, then `worker-check --wait` (one poll before the edge served the new HTML). **PASS:** every build file exactly the staged `.br`, a 304, the redirects, a 404, and each of the four data files a ranged HEAD and `bytes=0-0` → 206, its closed ranges byte-exact, no Content-Encoding, its SHA-256 the `MANIFEST.json` pin (`nasa-http-jul95.parquet`: 9,719,404 bytes, `9bb2d937…`, 8 closed ranges).
