Measured on Apple M1, Darwin 25.6.0 arm64, machine `local`, Chromium 153.0.8010.12 headless=new, 1440×900 at DPR 2, cold (a fresh browser per run), **3 runs** per side, stop, and profile (sizing the stops, not the protocol), reference and throttled (CPU 4×, Fast 4G). gpui-kit 0.9.0, React 19.3.1, TanStack Virtual 3.14.19, Arrow 17.0.0, DuckDB-Wasm 1.32.0 (each file's `versions`). ## What changed

Nothing in the contenders. The spike (`spike/tail/`, `spike/tail-gpui/`, `spike/tail-run.ts`; throwaway) has the two sides and the runner:

- **The data:** `data/nasa-http-jul95.parquet`, built from the draft `prep-nasa.sql`, byte-identical to part A1's (SHA-256 `9bb2d937…0ca2`). Each side fetches it whole after first paint and registers it as a `File`.
- **The clock:** one worker module for both sides (`tail/src/clock.ts`). About every 16 ms it posts the running count and the post's stamp, catching up at most 100 ms after a gap (market watch's `Rate`). It sends ~51–55 batches a second.
- **The loop:** the page SQL (`tail/src/sql.ts`, the same text as the GPUI side's `tail_sql`) has one SELECT per lap the page crosses, with the time moved on by ⌊*i* / *n*⌋ spans and drawn as the canvas writes it. Checked in native DuckDB across the file's end, and on both sides past a lap at 100k (screenshots).
- **GPUI** (crate `tail-spike`): the tall table built `follow` over `seiza-duckdb`'s `QueryRows`, following from the open. It calls `set_rows` in each clock message's task, with the probes on, as the contender's are. It logs each `TailDrawn` with the page clock in the render that emitted it.
- **TanStack** (`dom.html`): React and TanStack Virtual over a 4,096-row window on the scaled scroller (spacer ≤ 2^24 px, V = s + D), following the tail. The tail pin is the rows read, and the visible tail pages are read bottom first, 1,024-row pages, 3 in flight, and a 64 MiB cache. A landed page is drawn in the next frame's callback (`flushSync`), and the frame's drawn tail is logged in the commit's layout effect. It ports the table's rule in brief: no re-anchor, no thumb mapping, and a wheel up lets go. Part C ports it whole.

## The file in DuckDB-Wasm

The 1,024-row page SQL at 40 places through the file, on the TanStack page, with the file registered as a `File`: **7.5 ms p50** (35 ms the first, cold), against 2.0 natively (part A1). Half a page (a grown tail's re-read) takes 5.5 ms. A page across a lap's seam, read as two SELECTs, takes 9.2 ms. 30,720-row groups hold up in the browser.

## The scroll back under the feed

A 300 px gesture up at 10,000 px/s after the window: following let go and the top row held still for 1 s, in every run on both sides at every stop and profile (48 of 48, and in each control). Its control is the same gesture with the clock paused, after End brought the view back to the tail.

- **GPUI's wheel often reaches no frame in `EventLatency` under the feed:** the reading was missing in 16 of 24 GPUI runs, and where it was there (8, the throttled runs among them) it ended on the moved frame or up to 11 ms after it (31.3 against 20.4). So GPUI's scroll back reads by its probe, the response metric's own GPUI guard: the event's timestamp → the presentation of the first frame whose `Probe` top moved. In the control (no feed) the two agree to the hundredth in all 24 runs.
- **Readings** (p50 over the 3 runs): reference, GPUI 16–20 ms (probe) against TanStack 7–13 ms (`EventLatency`, the compositor's); throttled, GPUI 19–24 against TanStack 1–17. The DOM's native scroll answers on the compositor, as on the other scenarios.

## After the review

The code review of the spike found two faults in the TanStack side that bias its readings, both fixed and re-run (both profiles, every stop, 3 runs; the hold at 10k and 100k):
- **The tail pin lagged a page:** with bottom-first reads, a tail page landing before the page above it moved the pin only to the upper page's end once that landed, so the drawn tail waited for the next re-read whenever it crossed a page. The pin now moves to the end of the last page the screen is read through. At the reference nothing moved beyond the runs' spread (p50 19–21 ms both times, p95 27–29 against 28–31); throttled, 10k's p95 fell from 74.9 to 55.0 ms and its frame share rose from 70% to 76%.
- **The hold held more than a frame:** holding a landing kept its page in flight, pushing back the next re-read (+24.9 ms at 10k). Holding only the draw didn't work either: the last landing's held draw drew the next page early (+0.4 ms at p50). The pin now sees a landing 17 ms late, and the reading moves by about a frame (+17.5, +19.1).

The verdict and the stops are unchanged: TanStack keeps up at every stop on both devices (p95 ≤ 55 ms), GPUI everywhere but a slow device at 100k.
