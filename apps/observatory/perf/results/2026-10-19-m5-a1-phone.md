# M5 part A1: the GPUI contender on a phone, diagnosed (2026-10-19)

A diagnosis, not a measurement: nothing here is a budgeted number. Emulation on Apple M1, macOS, machine `local`, Chromium (Playwright's) headless=new and headed, the release builds of both contenders through `vite preview`; real-device checks by hand on an iPhone 13 (Chrome and Safari, both WebKit on iOS) over the LAN (plain HTTP: not cross-origin isolated, so WebGL2, not WebGPU; behavior only, no numbers).

## What broke in DevTools' responsive mode

Chromium's device emulation misreports `ResizeObserverEntry.devicePixelContentBoxSize`: at an emulated DPR of 3 (Playwright's iPhone 13: 390×664, `isMobile`, `hasTouch`) it reports the canvas at 1× (390×664), headless and headed alike. gpui-pre-web 0.3.9 trusts it, so the backing store is 390×664 while GPUI scales by 3: the window it lays out is a third the size, drawn 3× zoomed (5 rows, one column), and a touch anywhere outside that third lands outside GPUI's window and does nothing. This is root `CLAUDE.md`'s known gotcha ("Headless DPR emulation breaks GPUI's canvas sizing"), and it holds in headed DevTools emulation too.

| Case (390×664) | Backing store | Rows drawn | Touch swipe up (40% of the height) |
|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone 13 emulation, DPR 3, as emulated | 390×664 | 5, zoomed 3× | nothing |
| iPhone 13 emulation, DPR 3, `devicePixelContentBoxSize` hidden (gpui-pre-web's Safari path) | 1170×1992 | 20 | `top` 0 → 281 (row 9) |
| Touch, DPR 1 | 390×664 | 20 | `top` 0 → 281 |
| Mouse only, DPR 1 | 390×664 | 20 | `top` 0 → 281 |
| TanStack, iPhone 13 emulation | — | 20 | `top` 0 → 263 (row 8) |

A sideways touch swipe scrolled GPUI's columns (hidden-box case). On the iPhone 13 the GPUI contender drew at its normal size in both browsers, bare and in the page's frame: the zoom is the emulation's, not a phone's. Android Chrome, which reads the box Chromium misreports in emulation, is not yet checked on a device.

## Touch input

gpui-pre-web 0.3.9 sets `touch-action: none` on its canvas and forwards touch pointers as `PlatformInput::Touch`; gpui core's recognizer (`gpui-pre-0.3.9/src/gestures.rs`) turns a pan into `ScrollWheelEvent`s with a touch phase, and a release into momentum. Vertical and horizontal swipes and flings work on the iPhone in both browsers.

The caveat, found on the phone: **a pan is locked to one axis for its whole length.** Past the 8 px slop the recognizer takes the dominant axis of the movement so far (`dominant_axis`) and locks every later delta and the momentum to it (`TouchGestureState::Panning { axis }`, `lock_delta_to_axis`); unlike the wheel's `OngoingScroll`, it never unlocks. A diagonal swipe moves one axis, and when the lock picks the other one the table feels stuck. The tall table receives the locked deltas, so neither it nor the contender can recover the other axis. TanStack's native scroller pans diagonally. It felt worse in Chrome on iOS than in Safari (same engine); not explained, not reproduced in emulation.

## Whose it is

- **The zoom in DevTools:** Chromium's emulation, met by gpui-pre-web trusting the reported box. Not a phone bug. Upstream candidate (gpui-pre-web): sanity-check `devicePixelContentBoxSize` against `contentRect × devicePixelRatio`, falling back to the latter when they disagree by more than rounding.
- **The axis lock:** gpui core's touch recognizer (gpui-kit's, upstream). Upstream candidate: unlock a pan whose direction changes strongly, as the wheel's filter does, or a free (unlocked) pan for two-axis scroll containers.
- **The contender and the component:** nothing to fix; the component consumes scroll events as gpui delivers them.
- **What the page says meanwhile** (root rule 4): on touch, the GPUI side scrolls one axis per swipe; in DevTools' device mode it draws zoomed (an emulation artifact).

## Reproducing it

Emulation: Playwright's `devices['iPhone 13']` on each contender's release build with `?measure`, a touch swipe through CDP `Input.synthesizeScrollGesture` (`gestureSourceType: 'touch'`), `__contender.state` read before and after; the hidden-box case adds `shared-web/perf/protocol.ts`'s `hideDevicePixelContentBox`. A phone: `npx vite preview --host 0.0.0.0` in each contender's `web/` and in the page's, opened at the machine's LAN address.
