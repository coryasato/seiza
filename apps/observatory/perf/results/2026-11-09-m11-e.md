# M11 part E: live tail on the page (2026-11-09)

Measured on Apple M1, Darwin 25.6.0 arm64, machine `local`, Chromium 153.0.8010.12 headless=new. These are checks, not a protocol run: the cold numbers the page shows are part D2's (`2026-11-08-m11-d2.md`), resolved at build time. No JSON files: `check` and `page --check` ran without `--label`.

## What changed

- The page shows live tail: its route, title bar, status bar, panel (a log-scale keeps-up chart, four cards at a stop, the soak's memory in the card no stop changes, the 100k note), description, and the galleries' card, reach row, and the rule's rows clause.
- The collection card shows the pauses' time in 10 s (beside it the longest pause and those in a late frame) on market watch too. Nothing re-measured: live tail's medians over the runs come from the resolver's new `of: "median"` over a `*` step (`shared-web/scripts/results.ts`), and `metric tail` writes `collectionTotalMs` and `collectionsInLateFrame` from now on.
- The readout's ring on the tail level (`docs/contender-contract.md`, "The readout's ring"), kept only with `?readout`. GPUI's ring moved out of `feed.rs` into `readout.rs`, which both levels build.

## This tab's Shown in on the tail level

`page --check`, 10k rows/s, no input, read once the frame's ring holds 50 samples (about a second of counts): GPUI 30.5 ms (81 samples), TanStack 24.7 ms (51). The cold runs read 20.8 and 21.6 ms to the presented frame; the tab's reading is to the frame that drew the count, taken in the replay's first seconds, and the check asks only that each side reads a number. Neither bare page keeps a ring without `?readout` (the control). The check first read the ring as soon as the status bar showed a number, and TanStack's held 4 or 13 samples, depending on the run: now it waits for 50 on both sides.

## A chunking surprise

Importing `TAIL_KEEPS_UP_MS` (a number) from `metrics/metrics.ts` into `readouts.ts` turned the metrics module from an entry chunk of its own (manifest key `../metrics/metrics.ts`) into a shared chunk (`_metrics-<hash>.js`, `name: "metrics"`); the same build with the constant written as a literal kept the entry. It's still loaded only after first paint, by the same dynamic import; `page --check` finds it by either key now.

## After the user's look at the page

From screenshots of the preview, three fixes. The load explainer's labels keep a column of their own (`minmax(9rem, 1fr)`), and live tail's values are shorter ("by range, then whole", "once the file is whole"); `page --check` now fails if a label wraps past two lines. The dial says "appended while the view follows the newest" once, in its explainer, and each stop's line says what differs: the rows a frame takes in at 60 Hz (~17, ~170, ~830, ~1,700). The gallery's drawing has eight rows, so the outlined newest row isn't cut off at the thumbnail's edge. Screenshots checked by eye; `page --check` passes (first paint 81.2 ms, limit 85.0, a busy machine; first-paint JS 85.8 KiB, limit 86.0: 0.2 KiB headroom).

The status bar's "Memory 0 MiB" and "Shown in —" from the same screenshots didn't reproduce: a probe of the preview read 11–14 MiB and a live Shown in at 1k and 10k, through flips, a stop change, and the open panel, with and without `ForceEagerMeasureMemory`.

Then, with the user: the log-scale chart labels each point past its line with its value (816 and 843 ms at 100k, 102 ms at 50k on the slow device), checked by eye in both themes; `page --check` checks the labels against the numbers and passes again (first-paint JS 85.8 KiB).

**Found on the way, not fixed here:** in installed Chrome (not Playwright's Chromium), `measureUserAgentSpecificMemory` doesn't attribute GPUI's WebAssembly memory to its frame. Live tail at 1k, after the user's path (from market watch, several stops, a scroll up): the GPUI frame's `JavaScript` read 2.4–2.5 MiB in Chrome against 11–13 MiB in Chromium for the same state, with no other entry holding the difference. So a visitor's status bar understates GPUI's memory on every scenario; the cold numbers (Chromium) aren't affected. For the memory session.
