# M10 part A1: market watch's feed, its path, and its fold (2026-11-04)

Measured on Apple M1, Darwin 25.6.0 arm64, machine `local`, Chromium 153 headless=new, 1440×900 at DPR 2, cold (a fresh browser per run), median of 3: a spike that sizes the feed's path, not a protocol run (the numbers that publish come from part D). Load average 7.3 → 2.4 over the first matrix (the machine busy at its start), 1.9 → 2.8 over the second.

Raw: `2026-11-04-m10-a1-feed.json` (folds `none`, `objects`, `typed` at 1k, 5k, 20k, 50k, reference and throttled), `2026-11-04-m10-a1-feed-folds.json` (`objects`, `typed`, `wasm` interleaved in one reference session), `2026-11-04-m10-a1-feed-tick.json` (a message per tick, the control, at 1k–20k: at 50k a first attempt's trace was too large for Node to read), and, after the code review (below), `2026-11-04-m10-a1-feed-review.json` (all four folds interleaved, reference) and `2026-11-04-m10-a1-feed-tick-review.json` (the per-tick control again), all from `spike/feed-run.ts` over `spike/feed.html`: the feed's worker (`spike/src/feed-worker.ts`) and a fold on the page (`spike/src/feed.ts`), drawing nothing. The Rust fold is `spike/fold/` (raw wasm, no wasm-bindgen: each batch copied into its memory, as wasm-bindgen's `&[u8]` copies).

## What changed

Nothing built in the contenders: the feed's path and each side's fold measured, to spec market watch.

## The feed

- **The worker:** 50 fictional instruments, a seeded mulberry32, each tick a trade (60%: the last moves a price tick or holds, leaning back toward its reference) or a quote (the spread redrawn); every tick a pure function of the seed and its index. `rate` ticks a second counted out about every 16 ms, a fraction carried, a gap past 100 ms catching up only that much (the bare host's `Rate`).
- **A tick:** 40 bytes (u16 instrument, u16 kind, u32 bid size, u32 ask size, u32 size traded, f64 last, bid, ask), prices as whole ticks over 100 (or 1,000 under 10) so each is the nearest double to its decimal. A batch: a 24-byte header (sequence, count, send time in epoch ms, generation ms), the ticks after it, one transferred `ArrayBuffer`.
- **The same bytes every run:** the first 2,000 ticks' FNV-1a digest was `593060a5` in every run (120), whatever their batches' boundaries.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1k | 100% | 52.1 | 0.24 ms (0.34) | 0.04 ms | 18 ms/s | 8.9 / 10.7 ms/s |
| 5k | 100% | 52.3 | 0.26 (0.36) | 0.06 | 20 | 9.4 / 12.1 |

Reference, the `objects` fold unless named (`-feed-review.json`, after the review's fixes). The worker keeps up at every stop with most of its thread idle, and the post → handler time is flat with the rate: one transferred buffer costs the same at 50 ticks as at 1,000.

**A message per tick, the control:** the main thread spent 35 ms a second at 1k, 134 at 5k, and 289 at 20k (against 11, 12, and 16 batched), each message a task of its own (post → handler 0.28 / 0.84 / 2.0 ms p50 and climbing; `-feed-tick-review.json`), and at 50k a first attempt's trace was past what Node can read as one string. A frame's ticks go as one message.

## The fold

Interleaved in one reference session (`-feed-folds.json`); µs a tick is the handler's time (decode and fold) over the ticks it took.

| Rate | `objects`: µs a tick / ms a second | `typed` | `wasm` (the copy included) | Collections in 10 s: `objects` / `typed` / `wasm` | `objects`: their total, longest | Garbage freed (minor) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1k | 1.46 / 1.5 | 0.94 / 0.9 | 1.07 / 1.1 | 3 / 1 / 1 | 2.2 ms, 0.85 | 0.2 MiB/s |
| 5k | 0.48 / 2.4 | 0.22 / 1.1 | 0.32 / 1.6 | 10 / 0 / 1 | 4.7 ms, 0.71 | 0.7 |
| 20k | 0.23 / 4.7 | 0.06 / 1.2 | 0.10 / 1.9 | 32 / 0 / 1 | 14.2 ms, 0.70 | 3.0 |
| 50k | 0.17 / 8.7 | 0.04 / 1.8 | 0.04 / 2.2 | 61 / 0 / 1 | 21.4 ms, 0.61 | 7.7 |

- **The idiomatic JS store makes garbage in proportion to the rate:** ~160 bytes a tick (a replaced 13-field row object; the array copied once a batch), ~1.2 minor collections in 10 s per 1k updates/s, each ≤ 0.85 ms. `typed` and `wasm` make none: their one collection in 10 s is the page's own (the `none` fold, reading headers alone, has it too).
- **The fold alone is cheap on both sides:** under 9 ms of main thread a second at 50k for the JS objects, ~2 for Rust. So the dial's cost will come from what each side does with a batch after its store, the drawing (React's reconciliation of the changed rows, and its own garbage, against GPUI's frame); that is A2's spike. The collections the store makes are short; whether they land in a frame that goes late is a frame-level reading, A2's too.
- **The folds agree:** `--agree` folds the same 20,000 ticks at 50k/s with each fold: `typed` and `wasm` hold the same store as `objects`, field for field; the control, `objects` dropping tick 10,000, differs.

## Experiments and controls

- **The throttled profile on an idle page reads faster, not slower:** at CPU 4× the handler's times were ~¼ of the reference's (`objects` at 50k: 0.04 against 0.16 ms p50), the worker's generation too, and the batches 16.9 ms apart against 22.6. Not the CDP session (rate 1 through it read as the reference) and not the clock (a 0.78 s busy loop read 3.18 s at 4× by `performance.now()` and by Node's wall clock alike). It scales with the rate (2×: half; 4×: a quarter), so the likely cause is the OS's scheduling of a renderer that draws nothing (efficiency cores, a low clock) changing under the throttler's pauses. So this spike reads only counts and ratios within a profile: the collections (61 against 63 at 50k, the same in both profiles) and `objects` at ~5× `typed` per tick in both. A2's pages draw; their timings get a control of their own (a fixed busy loop per frame, 4× slower throttled).
- **The garbage control:** `typed` (0 collections at every rate above 1k) and `wasm` (1, the page's own) against `objects` (3 to 61): the collection count measures the store's garbage.

## After the code review

Fixed after the first numbers (`-feed.json`, `-feed-folds.json`, `-feed-tick.json`) were taken, and re-measured: the send time was stamped before the batch was generated, so "transport" held generation (at 50k, 0.55 ms p50 then, 0.26 now); busy time counted a toplevel task and the devtools.timeline task nested in it twice and could read a thread pool's thread for the worker's (the main thread's is now `frameWork`'s busy time, the worker's its outermost tasks on its `DedicatedWorker` thread: ~58 ms a second at 50k then, 32 now; the per-tick control's 69 / 251 / 552 then, 35 / 134 / 289 now); the Rust fold took batches of at most 4,096 ticks where a 100 ms catch-up at 50k sends 5,000 (now 10,000, and an oversized batch fails the run rather than dropping; no earlier run hit it: their one page error each was a missing favicon, now an empty icon); the drop control waited for one tick fewer than it folds; a page error now fails a run and `--agree`. The folds' per-tick costs, the collections, the digest, and the agreement read as before (`objects` at 50k: 0.20 µs a tick, 62 collections in 10 s, 7.7 MiB/s).

## Reproducing it

```sh
node apps/observatory/spike/feed-run.ts --label m10-a1-feed
node apps/observatory/spike/feed-run.ts --folds none,objects,typed,wasm --profiles reference --label m10-a1-feed-review
node apps/observatory/spike/feed-run.ts --send tick --rates 1000,5000,20000 --folds objects --profiles reference --runs 2 --label m10-a1-feed-tick-review
node apps/observatory/spike/feed-run.ts --no-build --agree
```
