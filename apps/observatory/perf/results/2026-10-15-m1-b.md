# M1 part B: the input-latency method (2026-10-15)

Measured on Apple M1, Darwin 25.6.0 arm64, machine `local`, with Playwright 1.63.0 and Chromium 153.0.8010.12 (headless=new), 1440×900 at DPR 2 with the DPR shim. Load average 7.33 → 1.69 over the latency runs (busy at the start; which frame each reading names doesn't depend on load, the milliseconds may), 1.79 → 1.94 over the work runs. No throttled run: nothing here is a budget.

Two pages: Tycho's release build (GPUI, the asteroids sample, observation panel hidden), on WebGPU and on WebGL2 (an init script deleting `navigator.gpu`; the backend checked from GPUI's GPU calls each session), and the spike's scaled scroller (the DOM: fake rows, the scrollbar shown). This sizes a method, not a comparison: the two pages differ in more than their UI, so their numbers aren't GPUI | TanStack numbers.

Raw (`just observatory spike --only latency,work --label m1-b`): [`…-latency.json`](2026-10-15-m1-b-latency.json) (3 sessions per side, 10 inputs of each kind per session, every reading, each session's page and console errors: none), [`…-work.json`](2026-10-15-m1-b-work.json) (10 cold runs per side, errors: none).

These are the last of several runs. A code review of the first found, and later runs fix: page errors dropped on the GPUI side; the latency probe's GPU-call wrapper left in the work runs (it cost ~0.03 ms per frame); the trace's clock taken from whichever mark came first rather than a known one (`trace:origin`, set right after tracing starts); the last input's window left open to the end of the trace; unequal idle tails after the two flings (now 500 ms each); and Tycho's fling sized by the spike's row count rather than the table's. One run then recorded no `workRecorder` frames (the one-shot backend hook restored the GPU methods it had captured under `workRecorder`'s wrapper; it now wraps last, and an empty recording fails the run). The last two runs added the compositor reading and the itemized frame.

## Four readings of one input

For each input: Chromium's trace `EventLatency` (the event's timestamp to the presentation of the frame it reached); the trace's `EventTiming` event (unrounded); the page's Event Timing observer (`durationThreshold: 16`); the probe, a mark in the first frame drawn at the new position (GPUI: after the task that made its GPU calls, reading `__tychoTable.top`; DOM: in a rAF, reading the row under the top edge), tied to that animation frame's `AnimationFrame::Presentation`; and, for the compositor's own scrolling, the first frame after the input whose compositor applied a scroll (`Scroll Delta This Frame`, category `cc`) to its presentation (the end of that frame's `SubmitCompositorFrameToPresentationCompositorFrame`). Inputs: Page Down/Up alternately, a press on the track (GPUI: 40% and 60% of it, the component jumps there; DOM: above and below the thumb, the browser pages with a compositor animation), a wheel event of ±300 px.

n = 30 per row; ms; "same frame": the readings end at the same presentation (within 1 ms).

- **GPUI's prevented wheel is in the trace:** `EventLatency` of type `MOUSE_WHEEL` (a second `MOUSE_WHEEL` without a `display_trace_id`, which reached no frame, comes with each), ending at exactly the probe's frame on both backends. Every GPUI reading of every kind named the probe's frame.
- **A native scroll reaches the screen before the main thread knows.** The DOM's wheel `EventLatency` ends one to two frames before the main-thread probe sees the move, and on exactly the frame the compositor first applied the scroll, 30 of 30. The control (the same page, the wheel prevented and scrolled with `scrollBy` from a listener) moves `EventLatency` to `MOUSE_WHEEL` and back onto the probe's frame, 30 of 30.
- **The DOM's track press, and a session's first one.** A track press is a compositor scroll animation. In 28 presses the main thread learned of the scroll in time for the press's own frame, and all readings agree. The other two were each a session's first press (sessions 1 and 2): the compositor applied the scroll in the press's frame (presented 7.9 and 5.0 ms after it, `EventLatency`'s end), and the main thread learned of it 19 ms later, so the probe was late, not `EventLatency`. The compositor reading agrees with `EventLatency` in all 30. A cold run measures exactly such a first press, so on the DOM the main-thread probe can't be the guard: the compositor reading is.
- **Event Timing, as a page reads it,** rounds to 8 ms (off by up to 3.9 ms from the unrounded duration here) and leaves out every event under 16 ms (3 of the DOM's 60 keys and presses here). The trace's `EventTiming` duration equals `EventLatency` exactly, for keys and presses alike. The latency runs keep GPUI's probe hooked on every GPU call; its cost (~0.03 ms per frame) is in their GPUI numbers.

## Per-frame work

The harness's fling on Tycho (WebGPU, `workRecorder` installed and no other GPU-call wrapper) and the spike's steady fling (180 equal events), 10 cold runs each, alternated, traced with the categories above plus `blink` (which names a frame's parts and the tasks outside frames). On the page's main thread: each `ProxyMain::BeginMainFrame` (the frame's whole rendering update) and its parts, the `FireAnimationFrame` callbacks inside it, and every task's busy time. Each `workRecorder` sample is paired with the `BeginMainFrame` that holds it (every sample paired, 10 of 10 runs).

The two flings differ (Tycho: the harness's, ~3 s, its last event the catch-up; the spike: 180 equal events, one per ~2 frames), and so do the pages, so the sides' numbers aren't a comparison; each shows what the reading sees.

- **The instrument against `workRecorder`:** the trace's rAF time agrees with it within the run's spread (p50 0.02 ms against a 0.07 ms spread, p95 0.02 against 0.55). The whole frame adds 0.41 ms at p50, itemized: the input dispatched in the frame and the update after the callbacks, which `workRecorder` doesn't time. With the latency probe's GPU-call wrapper left in (the first run), rAF − recorder was 0.05 ms.
- **The frame's parts sum to it.** Means of parts and medians of the whole don't add exactly (5.46 ms of mean parts against a 5.65 ms median frame); per frame they do, by construction (`other` is the frame less its named parts).
- **Work outside any frame:** ~8% of the main thread's busy time on both sides runs in no frame. On GPUI it's mostly the engine's page results arriving (read in wasm, in the message's task) and timers; the TanStack contender's results arrive the same way, so its decode will land outside frames too. Per-frame work doesn't see it; the busy total does. The tasks with no named event are script the categories here don't name; a one-run trace with `v8` and more showed most of the DOM's as microtask checkpoints.
- **Tycho's own work per frame** (`workRecorder` and the panel's row) is the rAF callback only, ~0.4 ms under this reading on the same fling: its numbers don't compare with the observatory's.
