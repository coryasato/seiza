# M9 part A2: the wide sheet's spike, both sides at six densities (2026-11-02)

Measured on Apple M1, Darwin 25.6.0 arm64, machine `local`, Chromium 153 headless=new, 1440×900 at DPR 2, cold (a fresh browser per run), **3 runs a setting** (sizing, not the protocol's 10), reference, plus one throttled run (CPU 4×, Fast 4G) at two settings. Load average 2.9–5.5 (the machine in use; builds between the runs). The medians are the reading; where a run strayed, the brackets in the raw files show it.

Raw:
- `2026-11-02-m9-a2-spike-gesture-tanstack.json`, `-gesture-gpui.json`: both sides on one input path, Chromium's gesture scroll (`spike/wide-gesture.ts`). **The comparison.**
- `2026-11-02-m9-a2-spike-tanstack.json` (`spike/wide-run.ts`) and `2026-11-02-m9-a2-spike-gpui-<row>-<text>-<width>.json` (the bare host's `perf.ts`, `--shape wide --cells heat --pin 2 --density …`, moved here from the table's results with their `label` fields as written): both sides under Playwright's bare `mouse.wheel`, the protocol's input until now. Kept for the finding below, not the comparison.

## What was built

Nothing that ships. `spike/` (throwaway) gains the wide grid: `wide.html` + `src/wide.ts` + `src/wide.css`, TanStack Virtual with a column virtualizer beside the rows' (overscan 4 rows, 2 columns), the row-number gutter and the two key columns (Station, Place) pinned by `position: sticky` inside the absolutely placed, translated rows, heat cells by `color-mix(in oklab, var(--chart-1) t, var(--background))` with the text's contrast picked past the middle, rows memoized by their row and column range; at `?row=&text=&width=` (the lengths scaled with the text as `density`'s are: padding 8 px at 14 px text, a key column 160 px). Its data, `spike/wide-data.ts`: the wide sheet's GHCN file (A1's pick, 10,632 × 365) read by native DuckDB into `public/wide/` (Float32, NaN for NULL; gitignored), held in memory, so a run reads the grid, not the engine. The GPUI side is the tall table's bare host built `custom-cells,pin,density` (`just tall-table build custom-cells,pin,density`): 1,567,523 rows × 500 float columns from memory, two pinned, heat, at the same row height, text, and width.

The cells on screen differ a little per setting: the spike pins two text columns at 160 px scaled, the bare host two float columns at the setting's width (308 against 308 at the base, 624 against 663, 817 against 840, 1,056 against 1,128, 1,512 against 1,643). The tables name a setting by the spike's count.

## The pinned edge and the header, in three browsers

`wide-run.ts --check`, at 16 px rows, 10 px text, 44 px columns: a vertical fling (top to bottom in ~3 s) and a sideways fling (first day to last), the header's top and the last pinned cell's right edge read every rAF against the scroller's box, and a screenshot mid-fling and at rest (`raw/wide-spike/`). In Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit both held to the pixel through both flings (header top 0, pinned edge 277 px, in every rAF); the screenshots mid-fling show the sticky cells over the scrolled ones and no gap. Firefox's vertical fling stopped at 157,282 of 169,262 px, as its cap of one page per wheel event predicts (`CLAUDE.md`, "Native scrollers under Playwright"). WebKit ran 55 rAFs in the vertical fling against Chromium's 173.

## The finding: a bare wheel reaches a native scroller every other frame

Under Playwright's `mouse.wheel` (raw deltas, every 16 ms, unawaited, as the metric scripts send them), the DOM side rendered new rows on **~29 frames a second at every density**, its frame work alternating between a frame that renders (7 ms at 308 cells) and an idle one (~0.6 ms). Its p50 per frame therefore read 0.7–1 ms and said nothing. The cause isn't the grid:

| Control (TanStack, reference, 1 run) | Rendered frames/s | Compositor frames that applied a scroll /s | Work p50 of a rendered frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 42 cells (60 px rows, 600 px columns, nothing pinned, text): bare wheels | 29.6 | 29.8 | 4.25 ms |
| 308 cells: bare wheels | 29.6 | 29.6 | 7.13 |
| 308 cells: the same 300 px a frame from a rAF loop in the page, no input | 59.0 | — | 4.92 |
| 1,056 cells: the same, rAF loop | 59.3 | — | 8.94 |
| 308 cells: Chromium's gesture scroll (`Input.synthesizeScrollGesture`, mouse source) | 57.6 | 58.1 | 5.29 |

The compositor itself applied a scroll only every other frame under bare wheels, at 42 cells as at 308, while the main thread ran a frame every vsync. A phased gesture reaches it every frame. The GPUI side takes wheel events on its canvas's own listener and drew ~59 a second under the same bare wheels at 308 cells (`perf.ts`), so this halves only the DOM side. Every TanStack wheel reading the observatory has taken (the spike's, the base level's fling and Smooth) went through this path: whether they move is a question for the protocol (`PLAN.md`, "Open questions"), not settled here.

## Both sides on the gesture scroll (the comparison)

`wide-gesture.ts`: three segments a setting, each a gesture from the top or the first day, traced on its own: **down** (37,500 px at 18,750 px/s, ~2 s: the bare host's steady scroll's distance and pace), **fast** (120,000 px at 60,000 px/s: a fling, every frame new rows), **sideways** (12,000 px at 6,000 px/s). The reading is the frames that drew new cells as a share of the display's frames (60 Hz) from the first of them to the last: GPUI's presented frames (`workRecorder`), TanStack's main frames that rendered rows (animate part over 1 ms). Work is the median of those frames' p50s, ms. Load average 2.1–2.9.

| Cells | Down: GPUI | Down: TanStack | Sideways: GPUI | Sideways: TanStack | Fast: GPUI | Fast: TanStack |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~310 (30,14,120) | 100%, 7.2 ms | 100%, 5.5 | 100%, 6.7 | 98.3%, 5.7 | 100%, 8.3 | **50.4%**, 9.8 |
| ~450 (26,13,96) | 100%, 9.7 | 100%, 6.1 | 100%, 9.2 | 100%, 6.6 | 100%, 10.9 | 50.4%, 11.2 |
| ~640 (22,12,80) | 99.2%, 12.5 | **70.8%**, 8.4 | 99.2%, 12.0 | 95.9%, 7.6 | 98.4%, 14.1 | 50.4%, 14.3 |
| ~830 (20,11,68) | 98.4%, 15.5 | 50.4%, 12.4 | 99.2%, 14.5 | 95.0%, 8.4 | **86.1%**, 18.3 | 51.2%, 17.4 |
| ~1,090 (18,11,56) | **77.0%**, 20.7 | 50.0%, 14.4 | **80.5%**, 19.7 | **52.9%**, 11.9 | 67.5%, 24.0 | 49.6%, 19.7 |
| ~1,550 (16,10,44) | 50.8%, 31.6 | 34.4%, 24.1 | 54.0%, 29.9 | 50.4%, 14.7 | 46.0%, 34.7 | 48.8%, 24.3 |

Bold: the first setting where a side drew under 90%. The three runs of each cell agree within 2 points but for two: TanStack down at ~640 (64.2, 70.8, 73.3) and at ~830 (50.4, 50.0, 87.6), the edge where a run holds or doesn't. TanStack's frames that didn't render rows weren't dropped: the compositor presented them **in part** (moved, the rows not re-rendered), 18–40 a second where it fell behind, 0–3 where it kept up; dropped frames were 0 at every setting.

- **GPUI draws ≥ 98% of frames to ~830 cells down and sideways, and to ~640 in the fast fling.** Its work grows ~16 µs a cell down (7.2 → 15.5 ms over 308 → 840 of its cells), near the table's density results (~14). Past the frame it presents fewer frames, every one whole.
- **TanStack draws every frame to ~450 cells down, 71% at ~640 and half from ~830; sideways ≥ 95% to ~830; in the fast fling, half from the base up.** Its main thread costs less per frame wherever it keeps up (~5–6 µs a cell down and sideways: only the rows or columns that came into view render, where GPUI redraws every cell every frame), and the fast fling's frames were only 9.8 ms at ~310 cells, so its half rate there is past the main thread (a screen of new content each frame at DPR 2: commit, raster, and the GPU; not split here).
- **Sideways separates them only at ~1,090**, where both fall under. Down and the fast fling do.

A first pass of this sweep read 0 frames for GPUI in 6 of 36 segments: the script's rest kept its state from the segment before, keyed by a count of frames, so a segment drawing as many frames as the last ended at once with a window before its start. The rest is keyed by the segment's start now, a segment drawing fewer than two frames fails the run, and the sweep was re-run on both sides; the first pass's files were overwritten.

## The bare wheels, for the record (`wide-run.ts`, `perf.ts`)

Under bare wheels the GPUI side matched the table's density results: the steady scroll presented 58.8/s at 308, 56.8 at 462, 36.9 at 663, 30.1 at 840, 29.9 at 1,128, 20.2 at 1,643; sideways 59 to 840, 45.2 at 1,128. Its vertical fling isn't comparable: the bare host's rows land a frame after they're asked for, so 30–83% of its fling frames held placeholder rows (cheap to draw), which no gesture segment had. The TanStack side's rendered frames, all at ~29/s: work p50 7.0 ms at 308 to 18.7 at 1,512 down, 9.4 to 23.7 in the fling, 6.6 to 14.7 sideways.

## Reproducing it

```sh
npm install --prefix apps/observatory/spike --no-audit --no-fund
node apps/observatory/spike/wide-data.ts <dir>/ghcn-2025-tmax-double.parquet   # A1's file
node apps/observatory/spike/wide-run.ts --check                              # builds spike/dist; three browsers
node apps/observatory/spike/wide-run.ts --no-build --runs 3 --label m9-a2-spike-tanstack
node apps/observatory/spike/wide-run.ts --no-build --runs 1 --settings "30,14,120;18,11,56" --raf --gesture
node apps/observatory/spike/wide-run.ts --no-build --runs 1 --settings 60,14,600 --pin 0 --cells text
just tall-table build custom-cells,pin,density
for s in 30,14,120 26,13,96 22,12,80 20,11,68 18,11,56 16,10,44; do
  just tall-table perf --shape wide --profile reference --runs 3 --cells heat --pin 2 --density $s --label m9-a2-spike-gpui-$s
done
node apps/observatory/spike/wide-gesture.ts --side tanstack --runs 3 --label m9-a2-spike-gesture-tanstack
node apps/observatory/spike/wide-gesture.ts --side gpui --runs 3 --label m9-a2-spike-gesture-gpui
node apps/observatory/spike/wide-gesture.ts --side gpui --runs 1 --profile throttled --settings "30,14,120;22,12,80" --label m9-a2-spike-gesture-gpui-throttled   # and --side tanstack
```
