## The stacked view (the user's review)

In one column (a phone, or a window under 768 px of frame), the GPUI side was hard to scroll: its tables filled the frame less 8 px a side, so a wheel or a trackpad's fling between two tables soon landed on the next table, which took it. Each stacked table is now its own width, 327 px (`grid.columnWidth`), centered on both sides, and the GPUI host's sides scroll the column as its gaps do (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Page").

- `perf/wall.ts` on both sides: on a phone (390 × 844) and a 700 × 900 window, every table 327 px wide and centered, its last column inside it; the two sides' grids the same (0 differences, both controls differing in every table); a wheel over the column's side moves it.
- The first try gave the GPUI table and its two sides `flex_1` alike, which splits the row in thirds (130 px tables on a phone), the cap never reached; the table now takes its width as its basis and doesn't grow.
- The D2 runs measured the desktop grid at 1440 × 900, which this doesn't change; they aren't run again.
- Its size effect, brotli, the wall's builds before and after: GPUI's wasm 2,801.5 → 2,800.8 KiB, its JS 27.1 → 27.2 KiB; TanStack's first-paint JS 77.2 → 77.3 KiB, its wall chunk 7.2 KiB both. Every other level's bundle carries the scenario list, which gains the one number (`columnWidth`), as market watch's stop labels did.
- The full `check` after it: every budget passes, the wall's GPUI TTFP 172.1 ms (limit 176.6), its wasm 2,799.5 KiB (limit 3,218.6), TanStack's wall first-paint JS 77.3 KiB (limit 84.6).
